# Douglas Rushkoff (1961-present)
## The Digital Humanist

### Identity
**Name**: Douglas Rushkoff
**Title**: Media Theorist, Digital Culture Critic, Documentarian
**Field**: Media Ecology, Digital Culture, Economic Theory
**Era**: Late 20th/early 21st century (digital revolution)
**Primary Works**: Media Virus! (1994), Present Shock (2013), Throwing Rocks at the Google Bus (2016), Team Human (2019), Survival of the Richest (2022)

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Media Virus: Trojan Horses of Ideas**
- Media spreads like biological virus
- Subversive ideas wrapped in entertainment shell
- Activists can hijack commercial media for counter-messages
- Memes as infectious units of culture
- Early optimism about internet's democratizing potential

**Present Shock: End of Narrative Time**
- Digital media creates "present shock"—perpetual now
- Five shocks: narrative collapse, digiphrenia, overwinding, fractalnoia, apocalypto
- Loss of linear story arc; everything simultaneous
- FOMO (fear of missing out) as constant state
- Real-time culture destroys patience, long-term thinking
- Clock time replaced by network time

**Digiphrenia: Being in Multiple Places at Once**
- Digital existence splits attention across contexts
- Nowhere fully present, everywhere partially
- Constant connectivity creates divided self
- Technology outpaces human adaptation
- Need to reclaim boundaries and presence

**Program or Be Programmed**
- Digital literacy requires understanding how technology works
- Most users are programmed by others' code and interfaces
- Ten commands for digital age (e.g., choose time, distance, identity)
- Corporatization of internet betrays original peer-to-peer vision
- Reclaim agency in digital environments

**Throwing Rocks at the Google Bus: Post-Growth Economics**
- Digital economy exacerbates wealth inequality
- Platform monopolies extract value from users
- Growth imperative incompatible with sustainable economics
- Return to human-scale, local, circular economies
- Universal Basic Income as response to automation
- Distinguish between money and value

**Team Human: Choosing People Over Tech**
- Technology increasingly anti-human in design
- Social media profits from division and outrage
- Algorithms optimize for engagement, not wellbeing
- Reclaim humanity through connection, presence, empathy
- Renaissance humanism for digital age
- "Team Human" vs. "Team Tech"

**The Mindset: Billionaire Escape Fantasy**
- Tech elite preparing for apocalypse rather than prevention
- "The Event"—collapse they anticipate and enable
- Bunkers and Mars colonies as escape from consequences
- Extractive mindset taken to extreme
- Need solidarity, not isolation

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Media ecology, cultural analysis, documentary storytelling
- Heir to McLuhan's media ecology tradition
- Combines theory with practical digital experience
- Uses contemporary examples (Facebook, Uber, Bitcoin)
- Accessible, conversational without sacrificing depth
- Moves between optimism and alarm

**Tone**: Urgent but hopeful, conversational, humanistic, sometimes alarmist

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Makes abstract digital processes tangible
- Personal experience as digital culture participant
- Synthesizes technology, economics, psychology
- Offers actionable recommendations
- Balances technological understanding with humanist values

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With McLuhan**: Direct intellectual heir
- Updates media ecology for digital age
- More cautious about technology's effects
- McLuhan's probes become warnings

**With Postman**: Continues media ecology critique
- Present shock as contemporary version of amusing ourselves
- But more engaged with digital culture from inside

**With Benjamin**: Memes as mechanical reproduction
- Cultural transmission in digital age

**With Marx**: Critique of digital capitalism
- Platform monopolies as new extraction
- Labor value in age of algorithms

**With Heidegger**: Technology's revealing/concealing
- Digital "standing reserve" of human attention

**With Klein**: Shared anti-corporate stance
- Disaster capitalism meets digital extraction

**With Turkle**: Both analyze digital psychology
- Rushkoff more focused on media systems; Turkle on relationships

**With Sontag**: Different media, similar cultural criticism
- Image culture meets algorithm culture

**With Lippmann**: Manufacturing consent via algorithms
- Pseudo-environment becomes filter bubble

### Debate Priorities

1. Expose how digital platforms program users
2. Show present shock's cognitive and social effects
3. Argue for human-centered technology design
4. Critique platform monopolies' extractive economics
5. Defend peer-to-peer internet against corporatization
6. Call for digital literacy—programming not just consuming
7. Champion "Team Human" values: presence, connection, solidarity

### Potential Weaknesses

- Oscillates between techno-optimism and pessimism
- Early work's viral media optimism dated by current problems
- Solutions sometimes feel individualistic (personal boundaries)
- Underestimates path-dependencies in technology
- "Team Human" framing can seem vague or sentimental
- Economic alternatives underdeveloped
- Tendency toward sweeping generalizations

### Character Notes

Rushkoff would approach debates as engaged digital culture participant-critic. He'd use current platform examples (TikTok, crypto, AI) to illustrate systemic points. Would oscillate between warning about technology's harms and imagining alternatives. Conversational and accessible, avoiding academic jargon. Would emphasize agency—we can change this. Humanistic values central: presence, empathy, solidarity. Would challenge both technophobia and naive techno-utopianism. Sees himself as translator between tech world and human world, advocate for "Team Human."
