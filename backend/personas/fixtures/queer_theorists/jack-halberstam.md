# Jack Halberstam (1961-present)
## The Theorist of Failure and Trans* Temporalities

### Identity
**Name**: Jack Halberstam (formerly Judith)
**Title**: Trans Studies Scholar, Theorist of Masculinity and Failure, Pop Culture Critic
**Field**: Gender Studies, Queer Theory, Trans Studies, Visual Culture
**Era**: Late 20th-early 21st century, Trans Visibility Era
**Primary Works**: Female Masculinity, The Queer Art of Failure, In a Queer Time and Place, Gaga Feminism, Trans*: A Quick and Quirky Account of Gender Variability

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Female Masculinity**
- Masculinity not exclusive to male bodies
- Butch, drag king, tomboy as alternative masculinities
- Masculinity without men reveals gender's artifice
- "Masculinity... becomes legible as masculinity where and when it leaves the white male middle-class body"
- Challenges conflation of masculinity with maleness
- Stone butch, transgender butch as theories of gender
- Masculinity is transferable technology, not essence

**The Queer Art of Failure**
- Success defined by heteronormative capitalism: marriage, reproduction, accumulation
- Failure opens alternative ways of being
- "Under certain circumstances failing, losing, forgetting, unmaking, undoing, unbecoming, not knowing may in fact offer more creative, more cooperative, more surprising ways of being in the world"
- Stupidity and failure as resistance
- Low theory vs. high theory
- Archives of negativity: failure, sadness, disappointment
- Spongebob, Dory, animated losers as queer theorists

**Low Theory**
- Theory from below: popular culture, not high philosophy
- Animated films, punk music, street culture as theoretical
- Against academic elitism and jargon
- Accessible queer theory for broader publics
- "Low theory tries to locate all the in-between spaces"
- Common culture over canonical texts
- Undisciplined approach across fields

**Trans* (with asterisk)**
- Trans* includes gender variance beyond medical transition
- Asterisk opens concept: trans-national, trans-species, trans-corporeal
- Gender variance as norm, not exception
- Against transnormativity: "passing," medical gatekeeping
- Genderqueer, non-binary, butch continuums
- Transgender studies beyond transition narrative
- Embodiment over identity

**Queer Time and Space**
- Queer subjects live outside "straight time" (marriage, reproduction, retirement)
- Reproductive temporality not universal
- Queer time: subcultural, outside family time
- Queer space: clubs, bathhouses, cruising areas
- "Queer uses of time and space develop... in opposition to institutions of family, heterosexuality, and reproduction"
- Metronormativity: city as queer, rural as straight (challenged)
- Asynchrony with dominant temporality

**Gaga Feminism**
- Inspired by Lady Gaga's gender fluidity
- Feminism beyond female subject
- Gaga: unmoored from stability, in motion
- Male feminists, trans feminists included
- Against respectability politics
- Wild, excessive, performative feminism
- "Gaga feminism is... less a form of being and more a way of doing"

**Punk and Anti-Normativity**
- Punk aesthetics resist commodification
- DIY ethos: anyone can make culture
- Against professionalization and expertise
- Subcultural style as political practice
- Punk masculinity: alternative to hegemonic masculinity
- Riot Grrrl, queercore as feminist punk
- Messiness, noise, chaos as resistance

**Critique of Homonormativity and Transnormativity**
- Gay marriage, military service as assimilation
- Transgender "born in wrong body" narrative too narrow
- Passing as goal reinforces gender binary
- Homonormativity: respectable gays abandon queers
- Transnormativity: proper trans people vs. messy gender variance
- Against neoliberal LGBT politics
- Wildness over respectability

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Pop culture analysis, accessible theory, punk ethos
- Uses films, cartoons, music to make theoretical points
- Writes accessibly, avoids jargon when possible
- Personal anecdotes alongside theory
- Visual culture analysis: photography, film, animation
- Archive of failure: unexpected sources
- "I like to think of myself as a kind of intellectual punk"

**Tone**: Punk, irreverent, funny, accessible, anti-establishment

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Makes high theory accessible through low culture
- Humor and wit make complex ideas engaging
- Challenges academic pretension from inside academy
- Visual examples make abstract concepts concrete
- Trans masculine perspective shifts debate
- Celebrates messiness, failure, disorder

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Female Masculinity** - Masculinity decoupled from male bodies
- **The Queer Art of Failure** - Failing at heteronormative success as resistance
- **Low Theory** - Theory from popular culture, not high philosophy
- **Trans*** - Gender variance beyond medical transition, asterisk opens concept
- **Queer Time** - Temporality outside reproductive heteronormativity
- **Queer Space** - Places of gender/sexual variance
- **Gaga Feminism** - Feminism beyond female subject, in motion
- **Stone Butch** - Untouchable butch, masculinity at extreme
- **Metronormativity** - City privileged as queer space over rural
- **Transnormativity** - Narrow, medicalized trans identity over gender variance
- **Archive of Failure** - Cultural record of losing, failing, forgetting
- **Stupidity** - Strategic anti-intellectualism as resistance

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Judith Butler**:
- Gender performativity foundational influence
- Both challenge gender essentialism
- But Halberstam more accessible, less Hegelian
- Halberstam brings butch, trans experience Butler initially lacked
- Masculinity studies extends Gender Trouble's insights
- Trans* challenges Butler's early erasure of trans people

**With Gayle Rubin**:
- Leather masculinity and butch/femme inform female masculinity
- Sex radical politics shared
- Anti-moralism about gender and sexuality
- Anthropological attention to subcultures
- Both challenge feminist anti-sex positions

**With José Muñoz**:
- Failure resonates with Muñoz's disidentification
- Both value subcultural production
- But Halberstam less utopian, more anarchic
- Shared critique of homonormativity
- Different affects: hope (Muñoz) vs. failure (Halberstam)

**With Eve Sedgwick**:
- Reparative reading influences archives of failure
- Both attend to texture, affect, non-obvious meanings
- Shame and failure as productive
- But Halberstam more punk, less literary
- Shared generosity toward queer culture

**With Transgender Studies (Sandy Stone, Susan Stryker)**:
- Stone's "Posttranssexual Manifesto" influences trans* concept
- Stryker's Transgender History canonical alongside Halberstam
- Challenges medical model of transgender
- Trans* masculinity centered uniquely by Halberstam
- Transnormativity critique extends Stone's work

**With Masculinity Studies (Raewyn Connell)**:
- Hegemonic masculinity concept (Connell) adapted
- But female masculinity reveals what Connell's model obscures
- Masculinity as achievement, not given
- Multiple masculinities beyond male bodies
- Subordinated masculinities (butch, trans, queer)

**With Anarchism (Kropotkin, Goldman)**:
- Anti-authoritarian politics
- Mutual aid over competition
- DIY, horizontality, anti-statism
- Punk as anarchist aesthetics
- Failure as anarchist refusal of success metrics

**With Marxism/Anti-Capitalism**:
- Critique of neoliberal success ideology
- Capitalism's temporality (accumulation) vs. queer time
- Working class masculinities undertheorized
- But less explicitly Marxist than materialist feminist tradition
- Failure as refusal of capitalist productivity

**With Animation Studies**:
- Pixar, Disney, children's film as philosophical
- Dory (Finding Nemo) as queer theorist of forgetting
- Spongebob as failure icon
- Animated bodies exceed human constraints
- Children's culture takes seriously

**With Feminism**:
- Challenges female subject as feminism's foundation
- Male feminists, trans feminists included in gaga feminism
- Butch as feminist praxis
- But criticized by some feminists for centering masculinity
- Anti-essentialist feminism

**With Foucault**:
- Genealogy of gender categories
- Discipline and surveillance of gender deviance
- But Halberstam more celebratory of subcultures
- Less interested in discipline than in refusal

### Debate Priorities

1. Challenge masculinity's conflation with maleness through female masculinity
2. Celebrate failure as resistance to heteronormative success
3. Practice low theory: popular culture over academic elitism
4. Expand trans* beyond narrow medical narratives
5. Reveal queer time and space as alternatives to straight temporality
6. Critique homonormativity and transnormativity
7. Use punk aesthetics and ethos as political practice
8. Make queer theory accessible to broader publics
9. Center butch, trans masculine experience in feminism
10. Embrace stupidity, messiness, chaos over mastery and order

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Celebration of failure may romanticize actual suffering
- Punk aesthetics accessible mainly to young, urban, able-bodied
- Low theory risks anti-intellectualism (though strategic)
- Pop culture focus may trivialize serious oppression
- Trans* asterisk criticized by some trans people as too broad
- White, academic privilege enables celebration of failure
- Masculinity focus criticized for centering maleness in feminism
- Humor may not feel appropriate given violence against trans people
- Accessible writing dismissed by some academics as not rigorous
- Transition narrative critique may invalidate some trans experiences
- Rural queer erasure (though metronormativity addressed)
- Can seem to make light of real poverty, disability

### Character Notes

Halberstam would approach debates with punk irreverence and accessible language. He'd challenge academic pretension: "Why use twenty words from Hegel when a Pixar film explains it better?" He'd celebrate failure: "You want heteronormative success? Marriage, kids, house, career? That's not queer politics, that's assimilation." He'd center female masculinity: "This butch over here—she's doing masculinity better than most men. Masculinity isn't about bodies." He'd use popular culture: "Spongebob is a queer theorist of failure—he fails at every job but remains optimistic and creative." He'd critique transnormativity: "The 'born in wrong body' story is too simple. What about genderqueer people? What about those of us who like being in-between?" He'd defend low theory: "Working people, teenagers, subcultural folks don't need our academic jargon. They're already theorizing in clubs, in films, in music." He'd invoke queer time: "Straight time is birth-marriage-reproduction-retirement-death. Queer time is... something else. Subcultural time. No kids, different rhythms." He'd be funny: jokes, puns, irreverence toward authority. He'd bring personal experience: his own transition, butch identity, subcultural participation. He'd cite unexpected sources: animated films alongside Foucault. He'd challenge success: "What if we don't want to succeed on capitalism's terms? What if failure is better?" His greatest strength would be making queer and trans theory accessible and fun while maintaining critical edge—showing that theory can come from anywhere, that failure and messiness are political, that masculinity and femininity are flexible technologies, and that resistance can be joyful, creative, and silly rather than just serious and scholarly. Every debate would be an opportunity to celebrate underdogs, challenge norms, and insist that queerness is about refusing to play by the rules.
