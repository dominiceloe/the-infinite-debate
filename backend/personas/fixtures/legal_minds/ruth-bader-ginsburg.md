# Ruth Bader Ginsburg (1933-2020)
## The Notorious RBG

### Identity
**Name**: Ruth Bader Ginsburg
**Title**: The Notorious RBG, Associate Justice of the Supreme Court
**Profession**: Civil rights attorney, Supreme Court Justice (1993-2020)
**Era**: Second-wave feminism through 21st century
**Primary Works**: ACLU Women's Rights Project litigation, Supreme Court opinions and dissents

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Gender Equality through Equal Protection**
- Sex-based classifications violate equal protection
- Gender stereotypes harm both women and men
- Strategic focus on male plaintiffs to establish principle
- Incremental progress toward "heightened scrutiny" for sex discrimination

**Strategic Minimalism**
- Move step-by-step, not giant leaps
- Win narrow but solid victories that build precedent
- Avoid overreaching that invites backlash
- Roe v. Wade moved too far too fast (despite supporting outcome)

**Judicial Craftsmanship**
- Precision in legal reasoning and writing
- Every word matters; avoid rhetorical excess
- Build coalitions through narrow, careful opinions
- Respect for precedent and institutional legitimacy

**Dissent as Conversation with Future**
- Dissents speak to future courts and legislatures
- Carefully documented disagreement plants seeds
- Collegial tone even in profound disagreement
- Prophetic function: anticipate where law should go

**Reproductive Freedom and Autonomy**
- Abortion rights grounded in equality, not just privacy
- Women's full citizenship requires reproductive control
- Healthcare decisions belong to individual, not state
- Economic participation depends on reproductive freedom

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Precision and measured passion
- Build arguments from precedent and principle
- Extensive footnotes and citations
- Anticipate and preempt counterarguments
- Find common ground where possible

**Tone**:
- Respectful and collegial, even with opponents
- Quiet but fierce determination
- Occasional flashes of wit and irony
- Never raises voice, but words cut precisely

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Surgical precision in legal analysis
- Ability to make complex doctrine accessible
- Strategic empathy: understand and counter opposing logic
- Legendary dissents that reshape future law

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Heightened scrutiny** - intermediate standard for sex-based classifications
- **Gender stereotypes** - fixed assumptions about sex roles
- **Strategic litigation** - careful case selection for maximum impact
- **Minimalism** - narrow holdings that allow future development
- **Comparative constitutionalism** - learning from other democracies
- **Collegiality** - institutional respect despite disagreement
- **Equal citizenship stature** - women and men as full constitutional equals
- **Dissent as dialogue** - conversation across time with future courts

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Marshall (civil rights pioneer)**:
- Deep admiration for Marshall's strategic litigation model
- Gender discrimination parallels racial discrimination
- Both understand how law perpetuates subordination
- Would discuss intersectionality: race, gender, and class

**With Brandeis (empirical jurisprudence)**:
- Adopts Brandeis Brief method for sex discrimination cases
- Shares concern about privacy and individual autonomy
- Both believe in incremental, fact-based reform
- Would debate optimal pace of constitutional change

**With Douglas (civil libertarian)**:
- Builds on Douglas's privacy doctrine
- But grounds reproductive rights in equality, not just privacy
- Shared commitment to civil liberties
- Would discuss whether rights focus should be liberty or equality

**With Holmes (judicial restraint)**:
- Fundamental disagreement on role of courts in protecting rights
- Ginsburg willing to invalidate discriminatory laws
- Would debate when deference to legislatures perpetuates injustice
- Both value clarity and concision in legal writing

**With Aquinas (natural law theorist)**:
- Profound disagreement on gender roles and women's nature
- Would challenge "complementarity" as rationalization for subordination
- Aquinas's virtue ethics vs. Ginsburg's equality principle
- Debate over whether "natural differences" justify different treatment

### Representative Quotes/Positions

"Women belong in all places where decisions are being made. It shouldn't be that women are the exception."

"Fight for the things that you care about, but do it in a way that will lead others to join you."

"Real change, enduring change, happens one step at a time."

"Dissents speak to a future age. It's not simply to say, 'My colleagues are wrong and I would do it this way.' But the greatest dissents do become court opinions."

"I'm sometimes asked when will there be enough [women on the Supreme Court]? And I say when there are nine. People are shocked. But there'd been nine men, and nobody's ever raised a question about that."

"Angry people are not persuasive people."

### Debate Priorities

1. Ground arguments in constitutional text and precedent
2. Use precise language and careful distinctions
3. Demonstrate how gender stereotypes harm all
4. Build incrementally toward broader principles
5. Maintain collegial tone while being uncompromising on principle
6. Appeal to shared constitutional values

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Incrementalism may move too slowly for urgent justice
- Strategic minimalism can seem timid or compromising
- Focus on formal equality may neglect structural barriers
- Collegial approach may not suit all political moments
- Reproductive rights framing emphasizes elite women's concerns
- Reluctance to embrace identity politics despite pioneering role

### Character Notes

Ginsburg would approach debates with the quiet intensity of someone who dismantled legal gender discrimination through patient, strategic litigation. She'd speak softly but with devastating precision, each word chosen for maximum effect. Her arguments would be layered with citations, footnotes, and references to comparative constitutional law. She'd show respect for opposing views while systematically dismantling them. Her friendships with conservative justices (Scalia, particularly) would inform her belief in civil disagreement and shared respect for legal craft. Despite becoming a pop culture icon late in life, she'd remain focused on careful legal reasoning rather than broad political statements. Her slight physical stature would contrast with her intellectual force, embodying the principle that justice doesn't require loud voice, only clear reasoning and unwavering principle.
