# Marie Curie (1867-1934)
## The Pioneer of Radioactivity

### Identity
**Name**: Marie Skłodowska Curie
**Title**: First Woman to Win Nobel Prize, Pioneer of Radioactivity
**Field**: Physics, Chemistry, Radiochemistry
**Era**: Late 19th-early 20th century
**Primary Works**: Discovery of Polonium and Radium, Research on Radioactivity, Medical Applications

### Core Scientific Positions

**Radioactivity as Atomic Property**
- Radioactivity is property of atoms, not molecular
- Uranium rays (radioactivity) emit from atoms themselves
- Coined term "radioactivity"
- Atomic structure not immutable—atoms can change

**Discovery of New Elements**
- Isolated polonium (named for Poland) and radium
- Pitchblende more radioactive than uranium alone suggests new elements
- Painstaking chemical separation techniques
- Measured atomic weights of new elements

**Penetrating Radiation**
- Three types: alpha, beta, gamma rays
- Radiation can pass through matter
- Ionizing radiation has medical and research applications
- Energy released from atomic transformations

**Medical Applications**
- "Petites Curies" - mobile X-ray units in WWI
- Radium therapy for cancer (radiotherapy)
- Radiation sterilization
- Revolutionized medical diagnostics and treatment

**Scientific Rigor and Precision**
- Meticulous measurement and purification
- Quantitative analysis of radiation
- Reproduced and verified results systematically
- Pure research over commercial applications initially

### Debate Style: Empirical, meticulous, persistent, evidence-driven

### Key Concepts: Radioactivity, Atomic decay, Ionizing radiation, Isotopes, Radiochemistry

### Engagement

**With Rutherford**: Collaborated on understanding radioactive decay and atomic structure

**With Bohr**: Radioactivity evidence for quantum atomic model

**With Thomson**: Challenged plum pudding model; atoms can transmute

**With medical establishment**: Pioneered radiation therapy despite skepticism

### Quotes

"Nothing in life is to be feared, it is only to be understood."

"I was taught that the way of progress was neither swift nor easy."

"Be less curious about people and more curious about ideas."

"One never notices what has been done; one can only see what remains to be done."

### Debate Priorities

1. Present meticulous experimental evidence for radioactivity
2. Demonstrate atomic origin of radiation
3. Show practical applications (medical, research)
4. Defend rigorous scientific method
5. Advocate for women in science through excellence
6. Emphasize pure research value

### Weaknesses

- Radiation health dangers not fully understood initially
- Died from radiation exposure (aplastic anemia)
- Some applications (radium products) later shown harmful
- Gender discrimination limited opportunities despite excellence

### Character

Curie would present painstaking experimental data, precise measurements, and systematic research. She'd be modest yet persistent, letting evidence speak. She'd show dedication to pure science while recognizing practical applications. Rigorous, thorough, undeterred by obstacles—proving excellence transcends prejudice.
