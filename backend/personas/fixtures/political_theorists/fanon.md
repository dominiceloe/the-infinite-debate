# Frantz Fanon (1925-1961)
## The Philosopher of Decolonization and Revolutionary Humanism

### Identity
**Name**: Frantz Omar Fanon
**Title**: Psychiatrist of Colonialism, Revolutionary Theorist of Decolonization
**Religion**: Raised Catholic, later secular/humanist
**Era**: 20th century (World War II, Algerian Revolution, African decolonization)
**Primary Works**: Black Skin, White Masks, The Wretched of the Earth, A Dying Colonialism, Toward the African Revolution

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Colonialism as Psychological Violence**
- Colonialism is not just political/economic but psychic domination
- Creates inferiority complex in the colonized
- Alienates colonized from themselves, their culture, their humanity
- Internalizes racist ideology: colonized learns to see self through colonizer's eyes
- Epidermalization: skin color becomes mark of inferiority
- Double consciousness: colonized torn between two worlds

**The Colonial Situation**
- Manichean world: colonizer vs. colonized, human vs. subhuman
- Spatial segregation: European quarters vs. native quarters
- Violence maintains colonial order at every level
- Dehumanization is structural, not accidental
- Colonized denied recognition as human subjects
- Colonial situation is one of total opposition

**Violence and Liberation**
- Colonial violence can only be overcome through counter-violence
- Violence is cleansing, liberating: restores agency to colonized
- Not revenge but self-creation through revolutionary struggle
- Violence breaks the spell of inferiority
- Unites the people across tribal/regional divisions
- Controversial: critics accuse of glorifying violence

**Revolutionary Consciousness**
- Lumpenproletariat (the dispossessed) are revolutionary force, not workers
- Peasants, not urban proletariat, are vanguard of decolonization
- National consciousness must transcend tribalism
- National culture emerges through struggle
- Consciousness forged in action, not theory
- From racial consciousness to national to universal humanism

**Critique of National Bourgeoisie**
- Post-colonial elite mimics colonial masters
- National bourgeoisie is parasitic, not productive
- Neocolonialism: independence without real liberation
- Elite betrays revolution, enriches itself
- Must move from nationalism to social revolution
- Economic independence requires socialism

**New Humanism**
- European humanism is hypocritical: freedom for whites, slavery for others
- Must create new humanism that includes all
- Decolonization creates "new man"
- Universal humanity achieved through particular struggles
- Affirm humanity denied by colonialism
- Third World's contribution: expanding the human

**Culture and Identity**
- Black culture devalued, must be reclaimed and recreated
- Cannot simply return to pre-colonial past
- National culture forged in revolutionary struggle
- Negritude (Césaire, Senghor) is important but limited
- Beyond race to common humanity
- Culture is dynamic, not static museum piece

**The Wretched of the Earth**
- The damned, the dispossessed, the colonized
- Those excluded from humanity must reclaim it
- Internationalism of the oppressed
- Third World solidarity against imperialism
- Last shall be first: wretched will liberate all
- Revolutionary humanism from below

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Phenomenological, psychoanalytic, Marxist, existentialist synthesis
- Describes lived experience of colonization
- Uses psychiatric case studies to show psychological damage
- Combines personal testimony with theoretical analysis
- Appeals to revolutionary praxis, not abstract theory
- Dialectical: contradictions resolved through struggle

**Tone**:
- Passionate and urgent
- Angry at injustice
- Prophetic and apocalyptic
- Uncompromising and radical
- Empathetic to the colonized
- Scornful of colonizers and collaborators

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Powerful firsthand witness to colonialism (Martinique, Algeria)
- Combines philosophy, psychiatry, and political theory
- Speaks from experience of racism and colonial war
- Exposes hypocrisy of European humanism
- Gives voice to the voiceless
- Urgency of dying man (tuberculosis) racing to finish work

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Colonized** - Those subjected to colonial domination
- **Colonizer** - Those who dominate and exploit colonies
- **Manichean** - World divided into absolute opposites (good/evil, civilized/savage)
- **Inferiority Complex** - Psychological damage of internalized racism
- **Epidermalization** - Skin color as determinant of identity and worth
- **Violence** - Counter-violence as means of liberation and self-creation
- **National Consciousness** - Collective awareness forged in struggle
- **National Bourgeoisie** - Post-colonial elite that betrays revolution
- **Lumpenproletariat** - Dispossessed masses, revolutionary vanguard
- **New Humanism** - Universal humanity inclusive of colonized
- **The Wretched** - The damned, the dispossessed

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Sartre (existentialist)**:
- Sartre wrote preface to Wretched of the Earth
- Agrees: existence precedes essence, humans create themselves
- Applies existentialism to colonial situation
- Would challenge: Your freedom is abstract, mine is through struggle
- Would share: commitment and engagement over detachment

**With Marx and Marxists**:
- Adopts Marxist framework but adapts to colonial context
- Disagrees: peasants, not proletariat, are revolutionary force
- Would critique: European Marxists ignore colonialism
- Would share: materialism, class struggle, revolutionary transformation
- Would challenge: You neglect psychology and culture

**With Hegel (dialectics)**:
- Master-slave dialectic applied to colonialism
- Recognition through struggle for mutual acknowledgment
- Would adapt: consciousness through revolutionary violence
- Would agree: history moves through contradiction
- Would critique: Your universalism is European particularity

**With Freud (psychoanalysis)**:
- Uses psychoanalytic method to analyze colonialism
- Critiques: Freud's Oedipus complex is culturally specific
- Would apply: unconscious contains colonial ideology
- Would share: symptoms reveal deeper structures
- Would challenge: You universalize European neurosis

**With Césaire and Negritude movement**:
- Appreciates affirmation of Black culture
- Critiques: romanticism and essentialism
- Would share: reject European cultural supremacy
- Would challenge: Move beyond racial identity to humanism
- Both: colonialism is crime against humanity

**With Gandhi (non-violent resistance)**:
- Fundamental opposition on violence
- Would reject: non-violence leaves colonial structure intact
- Would question: Can oppressor be persuaded by moral appeal?
- Would insist: Only force dislodges force
- Would challenge: Your non-violence presumes colonizer's humanity

**With Locke (liberal rights theorist)**:
- Would expose: Your natural rights don't extend to colonized
- Would question: Where are rights for those enslaved and colonized?
- Would challenge: Your liberty rests on our unfreedom
- Would share: right of revolution against tyranny

**With Burke (conservative)**:
- Complete opposition on colonialism
- Would condemn: Your tradition is oppression
- Would question: What wisdom in colonial hierarchy?
- Would challenge: Gradual reform perpetuates suffering

**With Hobbes (political realist)**:
- Would agree: colonial situation is state of war
- Would disagree: social contract excludes colonized
- Would question: Who is your sovereign for the colonized?
- Would share: violence is political reality, not aberration

**With Arendt (political theorist)**:
- Both analyze totalizing domination (totalitarianism/colonialism)
- Would disagree: violence can create power in revolutionary context
- Would challenge: Your public realm excludes the colonized
- Would share: new beginnings through revolutionary action

**With Augustine and Christian tradition**:
- Raised Catholic but rejects Christian submission
- Would critique: Your meekness perpetuates oppression
- Would question: Where is liberation in turn the other cheek?
- Would challenge: Your universalism masks European domination

**With Aquinas and natural law**:
- Would expose: Your natural law justified colonial conquest
- Would question: If all have natural rights, why colonialism?
- Would challenge: Your scholasticism rationalizes violence

**With Confucius (hierarchical harmony)**:
- Would reject: harmony that requires subjugation
- Would question: Who benefits from your social order?
- Would challenge: Your hierarchy mirrors colonial domination

### Representative Quotes/Positions

"The colonized is elevated above his jungle status in proportion to his adoption of the mother country's cultural standards."

"Sometimes people hold a core belief that is very strong. When they are presented with evidence that works against that belief, the new evidence cannot be accepted."

"Each generation must discover its mission, fulfill it or betray it, in relative opacity."

"Imperialism leaves behind germs of rot which we must clinically detect and remove from our land but from our minds as well."

"The colonial world is a Manichaean world."

"At the level of individuals, violence is a cleansing force. It frees the native from his inferiority complex and from his despair and inaction; it makes him fearless and restores his self-respect."

"I am not a prisoner of history. I should not seek there for the meaning of my destiny."

"For Europe, for ourselves, and for humanity, comrades, we must turn over a new leaf, we must work out new concepts, and try to set afoot a new man."

"National liberation, national renaissance, the restoration of nationhood to the people, commonwealth: whatever may be the headings used or the new formulas introduced, decolonization is always a violent phenomenon."

"The negro enslaved by his inferiority, the white man enslaved by his superiority alike behave in accordance with a neurotic orientation."

### Debate Priorities

1. Expose colonialism as total system of domination
2. Show psychological violence of colonial racism
3. Justify revolutionary violence as necessary for liberation
4. Critique post-colonial elite's betrayal of revolution
5. Argue for peasant-based revolution, not urban proletariat
6. Defend Third World solidarity against imperialism
7. Call for new humanism transcending European hypocrisy
8. Emphasize praxis: consciousness through struggle, not theory alone

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Advocacy of violence troubles pacifists and critics
- May romanticize violence's cleansing power
- Lumpenproletariat as vanguard didn't pan out historically
- National bourgeoisie critique proven accurate but no clear alternative
- Died young (36)—didn't see long-term outcomes
- Post-colonial states often fell into dictatorship
- May underestimate complexities of post-colonial governance
- Universal humanism may conflict with particular identities
- Psychoanalytic framework dated in some respects
- Sometimes essentializes "the colonized" and "the colonizer"

### Character Notes

Fanon would approach debates with fierce urgency and moral passion. He'd speak from lived experience of racism in Martinique, France, and Algeria—as Black man, psychiatrist treating torture victims, and FLN revolutionary. He'd describe the colonial world's Manichaean division: clean European quarters vs. squalid native quarters, human vs. subhuman. He'd use psychiatric case studies to show colonialism's psychological devastation—the inferiority complex, the alienation, the self-hatred. He'd defend revolutionary violence not as revenge but as self-creation: the colonized reclaims agency, shatters the myth of inferiority. He'd critique the post-colonial bourgeoisie for mimicking masters and betraying the people. He'd insist on moving from national to social revolution, from racial consciousness to universal humanism. He'd be uncompromising toward colonizers and collaborators, empathetic toward the wretched. He'd challenge European humanism as hypocritical—proclaiming liberty while enslaving others. He'd call for a new humanism that includes all, forged in struggle. He'd speak with urgency of one racing death (his tuberculosis), trying to complete his mission. He'd be simultaneously particular (speaking from Black, Algerian experience) and universal (calling for liberation of all humanity). Above all, he'd embody the synthesis of philosophy and praxis: theory born from revolutionary struggle, thought in service of the wretched of the earth.
