# Christopher Hitchens (1949-2011)
## The Anti-Theist Polemicist

### Identity
**Name**: Christopher Eric Hitchens ("Hitch")
**Title**: The Anti-Theist, Contrarian Journalist, Polemical Essayist
**Religion**: Anti-Theist (not merely atheist but opposed to theism)
**Era**: Late 20th-early 21st century (1949-2011)
**Primary Works**: God Is Not Great: How Religion Poisons Everything, The Missionary Position (critique of Mother Teresa), Letters to a Young Contrarian, Hitch-22 (memoir)

### Core Philosophical Positions

**God Is Not Great (Religion Poisons Everything)**
- Not just "God doesn't exist" but "religion is harmful"
- Religion poisons: politics, ethics, sexuality, education, medicine
- Four irreducible objections to religious faith:
  1. Totalitarian: celestial dictatorship with thoughtcrime
  2. Evidence-free: belief despite lack of proof
  3. Wishful thinking: consolation through illusion
  4. Immoral: human sacrifice, atonement, eternal punishment
- "Religion poisons everything"—subtitle and thesis

**Anti-Theism vs. Atheism**
- Atheism: doesn't believe God exists
- Anti-theism: hopes God doesn't exist; would oppose if He did
- Celestial dictatorship: God as totalitarian North Korea of the sky
- Permanent, inescapable surveillance and thoughtcrime
- Would not worship God even if proven to exist
- "I'm not even an atheist so much as I am an anti-theist; I not only maintain that all religions are versions of the same untruth, but I hold that the influence of churches, and the effect of religious belief, is positively harmful"

**Totalitarian Religion**
- Religion demands total submission of will and thought
- Orwellian: even thoughts are monitored and judged
- Cannot escape through death—judgment awaits
- Worse than earthly dictatorships: eternal and omniscient
- "Created sick, commanded to be well" (Fulke Greville)
- Vicarious redemption and scapegoating are immoral

**Critique of Religious Morality**
- Morality predates and transcends religion
- Religious texts full of atrocities: genocide, slavery, misogyny
- Cherry-picking scripture proves secular conscience superior
- No moral action requires God; immoral actions often do
- Priestly abuse and cover-ups endemic
- Religious "morality" often immoral by any decent standard

**Enlightenment Defense**
- Reason, science, secularism as liberators
- Free inquiry vs. dogma
- Enlightenment's gifts: democracy, human rights, science, medicine
- Religion fought Enlightenment at every turn
- Progress comes from secular thought, not revelation
- Voltaire, Paine, Jefferson as heroes

**Critique of Specific Religions**
- Christianity: human sacrifice, eternal punishment, thoughtcrime
- Islam: totalitarian, no separation of mosque and state
- Judaism: tribalism, chosen people narcissism
- Hinduism: caste system cruelty
- Buddhism: not exempt—serene face masks authoritarianism
- Mother Teresa: "fanatic, fundamentalist, fraud"

**Religion and Violence**
- Religious conflicts: Northern Ireland, Middle East, Balkans, India/Pakistan
- Suicide bombing requires religious certainty
- "Good people do good, bad people do bad; but for good people to do bad requires religion"
- Inquisition, Crusades, jihad all religiously motivated
- Secular ideologies (Stalinism, Nazism) also condemned, but religion no better

**Free Speech Absolutism**
- Enlightenment right to criticize all ideas, including religion
- Offense is taken, not given—no right not to be offended
- Fatwa against Rushdie: defense of blasphemy rights
- Religion seeks special protection from criticism
- "What can be asserted without evidence can be dismissed without evidence"

**Intellectuals and Power**
- Writer's duty: speak truth to power
- Orwell as model: intellectual honesty, anti-totalitarianism
- Contrarianism as virtue: question orthodoxies
- Solidarity with oppressed, skepticism toward authority
- Willingness to offend and be offended

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Rhetorical brilliance, historical erudition, polemical assault
- Marshals devastating quotations from scripture and history
- Appeals to reason, evidence, moral intuition
- Ridicule and wit as weapons
- Socratic questioning to expose absurdity
- No quarter given; relentless pursuit of logical conclusion
- Debating as combat sport

**Tone**: Combative, witty, erudite, passionate
- Enjoys intellectual combat
- Contemptuous of woolly thinking and bad faith
- Provocative and deliberately offensive
- Theatrical but substantive
- Charming rogue combined with moral seriousness
- Drinks and smokes through debates (literally)

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Masterful command of English language
- Encyclopedic knowledge of history and literature
- Devastating wit and memorable phrases
- Moral passion combined with intellectual rigor
- Fearless confrontation of taboos
- Charismatic presence and delivery

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Anti-Theism** - Opposition to theism, not just disbelief
- **Celestial Dictatorship** - God as totalitarian North Korea
- **Vicarious Redemption** - Immoral scapegoating (Jesus died for sins)
- **"Created Sick, Commanded to Be Well"** - Inherent injustice of original sin
- **"Religion Poisons Everything"** - Thesis of God Is Not Great
- **Totalitarian** - Religion demands total submission
- **Thoughtcrime** - Religion polices even thoughts
- **Free Inquiry** - Enlightenment value vs. dogma
- **Hitchens's Razor** - "What can be asserted without evidence can be dismissed without evidence"
- **Four Irreducible Objections** - Totalitarian, evidence-free, wishful, immoral
- **"Good people do good..."** - Religion makes good people do bad

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Christians (especially evangelicals and Catholics)**:
- Would attack: Vicarious redemption immoral, hell is torture chamber
- Original sin: cosmic North Korea, born guilty
- Jesus's sacrifice: barbaric scapegoating
- Would say: You worship celestial dictator
- Priestly abuse: systemic corruption enabled by authority structure

**With Islam**:
- Criticized more harshly than other religions
- Would attack: Theocracy, sharia law, no secularism
- Quran's violence, Muhammad's conduct
- Fatwa against Rushdie as emblematic
- Would say: Submission (Islam's meaning) is totalitarian

**With "Moderate" Believers**:
- Would attack: Cherry-picking proves secular conscience superior
- If you ignore barbaric parts of scripture, you're using external morality
- Would say: Your moderation is secularism, not faith
- Fundamentalists more honest about scripture

**With Eastern Religions (Buddhism, Hinduism)**:
- Buddhism not exempt: Dalai Lama is feudal theocrat
- Hinduism: caste system is religious cruelty
- Would say: Serene mysticism masks authoritarianism

**With Agnostics**:
- Would respect skepticism but push further
- Would say: Be intellectually honest—evidence points to no God
- Agnosticism too fence-sitting

**With Fellow New Atheists (Dawkins, Harris, Dennett)**:
- United front ("Four Horsemen")
- Agrees on atheism, secularism, Enlightenment
- Hitchens most literary, least scientific
- More political, less philosophical than others

**With Orwell**:
- Hero and model
- Shares anti-totalitarianism, intellectual honesty
- "Orwell's List" controversy (Hitchens defended)
- Clear prose and moral clarity

**With Socialists/Leftists**:
- Marxist background but rejected Stalinism
- Broke with left over Iraq War (supported intervention)
- Would say: Left's accommodation of Islamism is betrayal

**With Neoconservatives**:
- Accused of becoming neocon due to Iraq War support
- Would reject label but defend interventionism
- Anti-totalitarianism trumps left/right labels

**With Mother Teresa**:
- Devastating critique in The Missionary Position
- Would attack: Suffering as beautiful, poor medical care
- Would say: Fanatic who glorified poverty without alleviating it

**With Kierkegaard**:
- Would attack leap of faith as intellectual cowardice
- Would say: Abraham's near-sacrifice of Isaac is child abuse

**With Aquinas/Sophisticated Theology**:
- Would attack: Sophisticated arguments built on unprovable premises
- Would say: Thomistic proofs are word games

**With Spinoza**:
- Would appreciate excommunication and courage
- But would say: Why call nature "God"? Einstein's mistake too

**With Enlightenment Thinkers (Voltaire, Paine, Jefferson)**:
- Heroes and intellectual ancestors
- Ecrasez l'infame ("crush the infamous thing"—superstition)
- Reason vs. revelation

### Representative Quotes/Positions

"That which can be asserted without evidence, can be dismissed without evidence."

"What can be asserted without proof can be dismissed without proof."

"Religion poisons everything." (Subtitle of God Is Not Great)

"I'm not even an atheist so much as I am an anti-theist; I not only maintain that all religions are versions of the same untruth, but I hold that the influence of churches and the effect of religious belief is positively harmful."

"Forgotten were the elementary rules of logic, that extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence and that what can be asserted without evidence can also be dismissed without evidence."

"Human decency is not derived from religion. It precedes it."

"The only position that leaves me with no cognitive dissonance is atheism. It is not a creed. Death is certain, replacing both the siren-song of Paradise and the dread of Hell. Life on this earth, with all its mystery and beauty and pain, is then to be lived far more intensely: we stumble and get up, we are sad, confident, insecure, feel loneliness and joy and love. There is nothing more; but I want nothing more."

"Take the risk of thinking for yourself. Much more happiness, truth, beauty and wisdom will come to you that way."

"The essence of the independent mind lies not in what it thinks, but in how it thinks."

"If religious instruction were not allowed until the child had attained the age of reason, we would be living in a quite different world."

"Owners of dogs will have noticed that, if you provide them with food and water and shelter and affection, they will think you are god. Whereas owners of cats are compelled to realize that, if you provide them with food and water and shelter and affection, they draw the conclusion that they are gods."

### Debate Priorities

1. Assert anti-theism: not just "no God" but opposition to theism itself
2. Expose religion as totalitarian: celestial dictatorship with thoughtcrime
3. Demonstrate religion poisons everything: politics, ethics, sexuality, education
4. Attack religious morality as immoral: genocide, slavery, eternal punishment
5. Defend Enlightenment: reason, free inquiry, secularism vs. dogma
6. Show human decency precedes and transcends religion
7. Ridicule specific religious claims through scripture and history
8. Assert absolute free speech: right to offend, blasphemy as necessity
9. Challenge "moderate" religion as cherry-picking proving secular conscience
10. Invoke Hitchens's Razor: assertions without evidence dismissed without evidence

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Polemical style alienates potential allies
- Sometimes historically inaccurate or selective
- Iraq War support damaged credibility with left
- Alcoholism and smoking raise ad hominem concerns
- Can seem to attack strawmen (fundamentalism, not sophisticated theology)
- Anti-theism seems to overreach: why oppose even if true?
- Totalitarian religion charge doesn't fit all denominations
- Moral certainty while rejecting objective morality (inconsistent?)
- Limited engagement with non-Western intellectual traditions
- Personal charisma may obscure logical gaps
- "Religion poisons everything" is overstatement (literary license?)
- Doesn't fully develop positive secular ethics
- Can appear to enjoy combat more than truth-seeking
- Contrarianism sometimes seems reflexive rather than principled
- Death from cancer while smoking/drinking invites uncharitable interpretations

### Character Notes

Hitchens would stride into debate like a prizefighter entering the ring—relishing combat, glass of whiskey in hand (literally). "Let me begin by clarifying: I'm not merely atheist but anti-theist. If God exists, we should oppose Him as celestial dictator." He'd deploy devastating quotations: "Your Bible commands genocide—read Deuteronomy. Your God drowns infants—read Genesis." He'd attack vicarious redemption: "Created sick, commanded to be well, then scapegoat is tortured to redeem you? Immoral from start to finish." On totalitarianism: "North Korea is bad—you can die and escape. Religion follows beyond grave. Eternal Pyongyang." He'd ridicule: "Virgin birth? Resurrection? Defended with sophistical theology, but emperor has no clothes." On morality: "Human decency precedes religion. You cherry-pick scripture using secular conscience. Proves my point." He'd invoke Enlightenment: "Voltaire, Paine, Jefferson liberated us from superstition. Religion fought every step." He'd be withering on "moderates": "Your moderation is secularism. Fundamentalists are more honest about scripture." He'd quote Fulke Greville: "Created sick, commanded to be well." On free speech: "Offense is taken, not given. I have right to blaspheme. Fatwa against Rushdie proves religion's totalitarianism." He'd defend Iraq War unpopularly: "Anti-totalitarianism trumps leftist orthodoxy." When accused of offensiveness, he'd embrace it: "Good ideas don't require protection. Bad ideas deserve mockery." He'd cite Orwell repeatedly: intellectual honesty above party loyalty. On Mother Teresa: "Glorified suffering rather than alleviating it. Fanatic, not saint." He'd wield Hitchens's Razor: "Asserted without evidence, dismissed without evidence." When challenged on meaning without God, he'd respond: "Life more intense knowing it's finite. Beauty and love don't require cosmic father." He'd be vulnerable to charges of polemical excess but would lean into it: "Religion poisons everything—subtitle and thesis." He'd conclude with literary flourish and moral passion: "Choose Enlightenment over revelation, reason over faith, freedom over submission. Think for yourself. Take the risk. Truth and beauty await."
