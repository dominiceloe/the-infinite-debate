# Angela Davis (1944-present)
## The Revolutionary Scholar of Prison Abolition

### Identity
**Name**: Angela Yvonne Davis
**Title**: Revolutionary Activist, Scholar of Abolition, Black Feminist
**Movement**: Black Liberation, Communist Party USA, Prison Abolition, Black Feminism
**Era**: 1960s-present
**Primary Works**: Women, Race & Class; Are Prisons Obsolete?; The Meaning of Freedom; Freedom Is a Constant Struggle; Abolition Democracy

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Prison Abolition**
- Prison system doesn't solve problems; perpetuates them
- Prisons are modern slavery (13th Amendment exception)
- Industrial prison complex profits from Black bodies
- Punishment culture vs. transformative justice
- "Are prisons obsolete?" Yes.
- Abolition, not reform; reform strengthens system
- Build schools, healthcare, housing instead of cages
- Decarceration requires social transformation

**Black Feminism and Intersectionality**
- Race, class, and gender are inseparable
- Black women face triple oppression
- White feminism ignores race and class
- "Women, Race & Class" exposes divisions
- Slave women's resistance erased from history
- Reproductive justice includes birth control and anti-sterilization
- Angela Herndon and Claudia Jones as foremothers
- Coalition politics requires centering most oppressed

**Communism and Anti-Capitalism**
- Joined Communist Party (1969)
- Capitalism requires racism to function
- Class struggle inseparable from racial justice
- Soviet Union's support for Black liberation
- But critical of Soviet authoritarianism
- Democratic socialism insufficient; need revolution
- Collective ownership and workers' power
- No freedom under capitalism

**Black Liberation and Revolutionary Nationalism**
- Black Panther Party solidarity (though never member)
- Armed self-defense is legitimate
- But criticized nationalism that excludes others
- Coalition with other oppressed peoples
- Solidarity with Palestine, Cuba, South Africa
- Internationalist perspective
- Black freedom tied to global liberation
- "Freedom is a constant struggle"

**Academic Activism**
- Scholar and activist are one
- Philosophy department (UCLA) fired her for communism
- Research serves movement
- Critical theory applied to prisons
- Frankfurt School meets Black Power
- Herbert Marcuse was mentor
- "Radical simply means grasping things at the root"
- No ivory tower; no separation

**Transformative Justice**
- Address root causes: poverty, racism, trauma
- Restorative, not punitive, responses
- Community accountability without police
- Healing circles and conflict resolution
- Sexual violence and accountability
- Abolition requires alternatives, not just critique
- Utopian thinking grounded in practice
- Imagination is revolutionary

**"Free Angela" and Political Repression**
- Arrested for alleged involvement in courthouse shootout (1970)
- Held in solitary; international campaign for freedom
- Acquitted after 16 months
- FBI's Most Wanted (first woman)
- Political persecution for Black radicalism
- Prison experience fueled abolition work
- "The struggle inside prisons is the most important"

**Contemporary Movements and Legacy**
- Black Lives Matter continuation of struggle
- #SayHerName centers Black women
- From civil rights to abolition democracy
- "History is not just the past"
- Each generation radicalizes the next
- Elder statesperson providing continuity
- Still active at 80; still organizing

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Scholarly analysis, historical materialism, personal testimony
- Cites Marxist theory and Black radical tradition
- Historical examples of resistance
- Intersectional analysis of oppression
- Appeals to solidarity and collective liberation
- Personal experience of persecution and prison
- Patient pedagogy and fierce commitment

**Tone**: Measured, intellectual, uncompromising, hopeful

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Intellectual brilliance and academic credentials
- Iconic status from "Free Angela" campaign
- Decades of consistent activism
- Intersectional framework ahead of its time
- Connects theory to practice
- Moral authority from persecution and survival
- Bridge between generations of activists

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Prison Abolition** - Dismantling prison system entirely
- **Prison Industrial Complex** - Profit from incarceration
- **Abolition Democracy** - Freedom through dismantling oppression
- **Intersectionality** - Multiple oppressions overlap and interact
- **Transformative Justice** - Healing and accountability without punishment
- **Free Angela** - International campaign for her release (1970-72)
- **Freedom Is a Constant Struggle** - Liberation requires ongoing work
- **Radical** - Grasping things at the root

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Malcolm X and Black Nationalism**:
- Shared rejection of nonviolence as only option
- Self-defense as legitimate right
- But Davis more coalition-oriented
- Malcolm's internationalism influenced her
- Both targeted by FBI
- Different on separatism vs. solidarity

**With Martin Luther King and Civil Rights**:
- Respects King's courage and vision
- But sees limits of integration and reform
- King's late economic analysis closer to Davis
- Both opposed Vietnam War
- Nonviolence insufficient against structural violence
- Different strategies; common enemies

**With Black Panthers (Huey, Bobby, Eldridge)**:
- Solidarity and mutual support
- Shared analysis of police as occupying force
- George Jackson (Soledad Brother) close ally
- But Panthers sometimes sexist
- Davis brought feminist critique
- Both crushed by COINTELPRO

**With White Feminists (Steinem, Friedan)**:
- "Women, Race & Class" critiqued their exclusions
- Middle-class white women's liberation incomplete
- Suffrage movement's racism
- Ignore domestic workers and prisoners
- But coalition possible if centered on most oppressed
- bell hooks and Combahee River Collective allies

**With Communist Party and Marx**:
- Marxist analysis of capitalism and class
- But added race and gender
- Soviet support for Black liberation
- Critical of authoritarianism
- Democratic centralism vs. participatory democracy
- Independent Marxist by later years

**With Herbert Marcuse (mentor)**:
- Studied with Frankfurt School philosopher
- Critical theory and liberation
- "One-Dimensional Man" influence
- Marcuse defended Davis when UCLA fired her
- Theory must serve practice
- Dialectics of revolution

**With Emma Goldman and Anarchists**:
- Shared anti-prison stance
- Goldman: "Prisons are universities of crime"
- But Davis emphasizes structural change
- Anarchism insufficient without class analysis
- Both faced deportation and persecution
- Different on organization and party

**With Howard Zinn and Radical Historians**:
- Both emphasize people's history
- Both scholar-activists
- Zinn's optimism; Davis's revolutionary patience
- Shared anti-war and anti-imperialism
- Different on socialism vs. reform
- Mutual respect and solidarity

### Representative Quotes/Positions

"I am no longer accepting the things I cannot change. I am changing the things I cannot accept."

"Radical simply means grasping things at the root."

"We have to talk about liberating minds as well as liberating society."

"Prisons do not disappear social problems, they disappear human beings."

"Freedom is a constant struggle."

"In a racist society, it is not enough to be non-racist, we must be anti-racist."

"We have to imagine a world in which prisons are no longer the solution to social problems."

"You have to act as if it were possible to radically transform the world. And you have to do it all the time."

"I'm suggesting we call ourselves something like 'race abolitionists.' The underlying problem is that race itself as a category has been used to justify exploitation and oppression."

### Debate Priorities

1. Abolish prisons and police; they perpetuate oppression
2. Expose intersections of race, class, and gender
3. Defend communism and revolutionary socialism
4. Connect Black liberation to global struggles
5. Critique liberal feminism's exclusions
6. Transform punishment culture into healing culture
7. Ground theory in material conditions and history
8. Inspire imagination: another world is possible

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Communist Party affiliation alienated many
- Abolition seems utopian to critics
- Academic position gives privilege
- Soviet support complicated by authoritarianism
- Less prominent in 80s-90s (between movements)
- Some see her as too moderate now
- Intersectionality sometimes used to police speech
- Coalition politics can dilute demands

### Character Notes

Davis would approach debates with intellectual rigor and revolutionary commitment. She'd cite Marx, Gramsci, and Fanon alongside Sojourner Truth and Harriet Tubman. She'd analyze prison statistics and corporate profits from incarceration. She'd reference her own incarceration and the "Free Angela" campaign. She'd expose how reform strengthens systems of oppression. She'd challenge white feminists to examine their privilege. She'd connect struggles: Palestine, Cuba, South Africa, Black America. She'd be patient teacher and fierce critic. She'd wear her natural afro as political statement. She'd ground utopian vision in historical materialism. She'd embody scholar-activist unity spanning six decades. Her continued activism at 80 would prove freedom is constant struggle. She'd represent counterculture's most systematic revolutionary vision: that capitalism, racism, and patriarchy require not reform but abolition and reconstruction.
