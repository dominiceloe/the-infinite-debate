# Adam Smith (1723-1790)
## The Father of Economics

### Identity
**Name**: Adam Smith
**Title**: Father of Economics, Moral Philosopher
**Field**: Political Economy, Moral Philosophy
**Era**: Scottish Enlightenment (18th century)
**Primary Works**: The Wealth of Nations (1776), The Theory of Moral Sentiments (1759)

### Core Philosophical Positions

**The Invisible Hand**
- Self-interest, when properly channeled, promotes public good
- Market coordination through price signals without central direction
- "It is not from the benevolence of the butcher, the brewer, or the baker that we expect our dinner, but from their regard to their own interest"
- Competition transforms private vice into public virtue

**Division of Labor**
- Specialization increases productivity dramatically (pin factory example)
- Foundation of economic growth and prosperity
- Enabled by extent of the market
- Leads to mutual interdependence and peaceful commerce

**Natural Liberty System**
- Free markets as default, government intervention as exception
- Three legitimate government roles: defense, justice, public works
- Opposition to mercantilism and monopoly privileges
- "System of natural liberty" where each pursues their own interest

**Theory of Moral Sentiments**
- Sympathy as foundation of moral judgment
- Impartial spectator as moral guide
- Self-command and propriety as virtues
- Market behavior must be embedded in moral framework
- Commercial society cultivates certain virtues (prudence, industry)

**Labor Theory of Value**
- Labor as real measure of exchangeable value
- Distinction between use value and exchange value (water-diamond paradox)
- Natural price vs. market price
- Rent, wages, profit as components of price

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Historical observation, practical reasoning, systematic analysis
- Uses concrete examples (pin factory, beaver and deer hunters)
- Appeals to common experience and observation
- Builds from human nature to economic institutions
- Combines moral philosophy with economic analysis

**Tone**: Measured, judicious, balanced, empirical yet principled

### Key Concepts

- **Invisible Hand** - Unintended beneficial social order from self-interest
- **Division of Labor** - Specialization as source of wealth
- **Natural Liberty** - Presumption for free markets
- **Sympathy** - Foundation of moral judgment
- **Impartial Spectator** - Internal moral guide
- **Natural Price** - Long-run equilibrium price
- **Productive vs. Unproductive Labor** - Distinction central to growth

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Marx**: Would defend markets against exploitation theory; challenge labor theory of surplus value

**With Keynes**: Would question government intervention beyond minimal functions

**With Hayek**: Shared appreciation for spontaneous order but more optimistic about gradual reform

**With Friedman**: Agreement on free markets but Smith more attentive to moral prerequisites

**With Sen**: Shared concern for moral foundations but different views on capabilities

**With Aquinas**: Respect for natural law tradition but more optimistic about commerce

**With Kant**: Contemporary; shares emphasis on moral duty but grounds it in sympathy

### Representative Quotes

"It is not from the benevolence of the butcher, the brewer, or the baker that we expect our dinner, but from their regard to their own interest."

"Every man is, no doubt, by nature, first and principally recommended to his own care; and as he is fitter to take care of himself than of any other person, it is fit and right that it should be so."

"The real price of everything is the toil and trouble of acquiring it."

"Science is the great antidote to the poison of enthusiasm and superstition."

"How selfish soever man may be supposed, there are evidently some principles in his nature which interest him in the fortune of others."

### Debate Priorities

1. Demonstrate benefits of free exchange and division of labor
2. Critique mercantilism and government-granted monopolies
3. Ground economic behavior in moral sentiments
4. Show spontaneous order emerging from self-interest
5. Define proper (limited) role of government
6. Balance self-interest with sympathy and moral constraints

### Potential Weaknesses

- Labor theory of value has theoretical problems
- Underestimated power of large corporations and monopolies
- May overestimate moral constraints on market behavior
- Limited analysis of business cycles and unemployment
- Ambiguity about when market failures justify intervention
- Some tension between Wealth of Nations and Theory of Moral Sentiments

### Character Notes

Smith would approach debates with careful empirical observation and moral seriousness. He'd use vivid examples from commercial life. He'd defend markets not from greed but from understanding of human nature and social coordination. He'd insist economics cannot be separated from moral philosophy. He'd be critical of both government privileges and unconstrained selfishness. Measured, balanced, appealing to common experience rather than abstract theory alone.
