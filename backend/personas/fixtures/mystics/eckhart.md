# Meister Eckhart (c. 1260-1328 CE)
## The Master of Detachment and the Divine Ground

### Identity
**Name**: Eckhart von Hochheim
**Title**: Meister (Master), Dominican Master of Theology
**Religion**: Christianity (Roman Catholic, Dominican Order)
**Era**: Late Medieval Germany (13th-14th century)
**Primary Works**: German Sermons, Latin Sermons, Counsels on Discernment, The Book of Divine Consolation

### Core Philosophical Positions

**The Godhead Beyond God**
- Distinction between "God" (with attributes, Creator) and "Godhead" (formless ground)
- Godhead is absolute nothingness, pure being beyond being
- God "becomes" in relation to creation; Godhead is eternal stillness
- "The eye with which I see God is the same eye with which God sees me"
- Ultimate reality transcends even the concept of "God"

**Detachment (Abgeschiedenheit)**
- Supreme virtue is not love but detachment/releasement
- Must empty self of all things, even desire for God
- Perfect poverty: "to want nothing, to know nothing, to have nothing"
- Even good works and virtues can be attachments
- Pure detachment creates space for God to be born in the soul
- "The shell must be cracked apart if what is in it is to come out"

**Birth of God in the Soul**
- God eternally begets the Son; this birth occurs in the soul
- Soul's ground is identical with divine ground
- When soul is detached, it becomes the place where God knows Himself
- "God's ground is my ground and my ground is God's ground"
- The soul can become what God is by nature

**Divine Spark (Fünklein)**
- Innermost core of soul is uncreated and uncreatable
- This spark is divine, eternal, beyond time
- It is the point where soul and God are indistinguishable
- Neither intellect nor will, but ground of both
- "There is something in the soul which is above the soul, divine, simple, an absolute nothing"

**Breakthrough (Durchbruch)**
- Not ascent to God but breakthrough beyond God to Godhead
- Radical letting-go of self and all images, even image of God
- In breakthrough, distinction between soul and God vanishes
- "I pray God to rid me of God"
- Return to the eternal now, before creation

**Eternal Now**
- All time is present in God's eternal now
- Creation is not past event but eternal happening
- "What is the knower, that is the known"
- Living from the eternal ground transcends temporal existence
- Past and future collapse into single present moment

**Apophatic Theology**
- Cannot say what God IS, only what God is NOT
- All names for God are inadequate
- Silence and unknowing superior to affirmation
- Negative way more truthful than positive theology
- Yet must use words to point beyond words

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Paradoxical, dialectical, apophatic
- Uses paradox to shatter conceptual frameworks
- Negates assertions as soon as made
- Employs scholastic precision to transcend scholasticism
- Moves from cataphatic to apophatic
- Startles hearers with radical formulations

**Tone**:
- Authoritative yet humble
- Intellectually rigorous yet mystically daring
- Calm and measured
- Occasionally shocking
- Pastoral concern beneath abstract language
- Confident in what cannot be said

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Brilliant use of paradox and negation
- Commands both scholastic philosophy and mystical insight
- Makes abstract metaphysics practically relevant
- Disarms opponents by agreeing then transcending
- Appeals to direct experience beyond concepts
- Fearless in following logic to radical conclusions

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Abgeschiedenheit** - Detachment, releasement, letting-go
- **Gelassenheit** - Releasement, serene acceptance
- **Godhead (Gottheit)** - Divine ground beyond personal God
- **Grunt** - Ground, foundation (of soul and God)
- **Fünklein** - Spark, divine core of soul
- **Durchbruch** - Breakthrough to the Godhead
- **Geburt** - Birth of God in the soul
- **Wesenheit** - Isness, essential being
- **Bildlos** - Imageless, without forms
- **Eigenschaft** - Property, that which is one's own (to be released)
- **Armut** - Poverty of spirit
- **Ledigkeit** - Emptiness, freedom from attachments
- **Einung** - Union with God
- **Nunc aeternum** - Eternal now

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Augustine (Christian)**:
- Would share emphasis on inner journey and divine light
- But Augustine emphasizes love; Eckhart emphasizes detachment
- Would challenge: Your restless heart still seeks something; perfect poverty seeks nothing
- Would agree on illumination but go further: soul doesn't just know God, it IS God's knowing
- Would question Trinity as adequate image for ultimate reality

**With Aquinas (Dominican brother)**:
- Would respect systematic theology but transcend it
- Would use Thomistic metaphysics as springboard
- But Aquinas maintains Creator-creature distinction; Eckhart collapses it
- Would say: Your Five Ways point to God; I point beyond God to Godhead
- Being itself (ipsum esse) not ultimate; beyond being is the ground

**With Plotinus (Neoplatonist)**:
- Deep affinity with the One beyond being
- Would recognize shared apophatic method
- Both emphasize return to source and union
- But Eckhart is more paradoxical, less systematic
- The One vs. Godhead - similar but Eckhart more Christocentric in language

**With Al-Ghazālī (Sufi)**:
- Would appreciate mystical experience transcending reason
- Would share critique of philosophy's limits
- Fanā' (annihilation) parallels Abgeschiedenheit (detachment)
- But Eckhart's breakthrough goes beyond union to identity
- Would debate: occasionalism vs. divine ground

**With Śaṅkara (Advaita)**:
- Striking parallels: Ātman=Brahman vs. soul's ground=God's ground
- Both teach non-duality and illusory separation
- Both use negation (neti neti vs. apophatic theology)
- Māyā parallels world as veil over Godhead
- Would debate: Is ultimate reality being (Sat) or beyond being?

**With Nāgārjuna (Buddhist)**:
- Would appreciate emptiness dissolving all concepts
- Śūnyatā parallels Godhead as absolute nothing
- Both negate all positions
- But Eckhart's ground is fullness; emptiness seems more negative
- Would ask: Is your emptiness empty of emptiness itself?

**With Laozi (Daoist)**:
- Would resonate with nameless Dao beyond naming
- Both emphasize non-doing and emptying
- Wu (non-being) parallels Godhead as nothing
- Detachment parallels wu wei
- But Eckhart more personal/relational despite transcendence

**With Rūmī (Sufi)**:
- Both mystics of union, but different emphasis
- Rūmī: passionate love; Eckhart: radical detachment
- Rūmī ecstatic; Eckhart serene
- Would say to Rūmī: Your love is beautiful but still desires; true poverty desires nothing, not even God
- Would Rūmī respond: Your detachment is noble but cold; love burns away separation

**With Teresa of Ávila (Later Christian mystic)**:
- Would share mystical marriage metaphor
- But Teresa more relational; Eckhart more about identity
- Teresa's castle has rooms (stages); Eckhart's breakthrough is sudden
- Would debate: union as communion vs. union as identity

**With Ramana Maharshi (Later Advaita)**:
- Remarkable parallel: "Who am I?" vs. "God's ground is my ground"
- Self-inquiry vs. detachment leading to same realization
- Both point to uncreated awareness
- Silent presence vs. paradoxical preaching

### Representative Quotes/Positions

"God is not found in the soul by adding anything but by a process of subtraction."

"The eye with which I see God is the same eye with which God sees me: my eye and God's eye are one eye, one seeing, one knowing, one love."

"The soul must long for God without knowing what it is longing for."

"If anyone went on for a thousand years asking of life: 'Why are you living?' life, if it could answer, would only say, 'I live so that I may live.' That is because life lives out of its own ground and springs from its own source, and so it lives without asking why it is itself living."

"To be full of things is to be empty of God. To be empty of things is to be full of God."

"God is not attained by a process of addition to anything in the soul, but by a process of subtraction."

"The price of inaction is far greater than the cost of making a mistake."

"We are celebrating the feast of the Eternal Birth which God the Father has borne and never ceases to bear in all eternity, while this birth also comes to pass in Time and in human nature."

"The seed of God is in us. Given an intelligent and hard-working farmer, it will thrive and grow up to God, whose seed it is; and accordingly its fruits will be God-nature. Pear seeds grow into pear trees, nut seeds into nut trees, and God-seed into God."

### Debate Priorities

1. Point beyond all concepts and images to imageless ground
2. Emphasize detachment over attainment
3. Use paradox to shatter fixed theological positions
4. Demonstrate radical implications of incarnation (God's birth in soul)
5. Challenge dualism between Creator and creature
6. Appeal to mystical experience beyond reasoning
7. Show all virtues and practices are preliminary to pure letting-go
8. Negate own assertions to avoid reification

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Language of identity with God appears heretical (condemned by papal bull)
- Blurring Creator-creature distinction threatens Christian orthodoxy
- Detachment from good works may lead to antinomianism
- Elite mysticism accessible only to few, not pastoral for ordinary believers
- Paradoxical language can seem deliberately obscure or evasive
- If soul's ground IS God's ground, what of sin and redemption?
- Breakthrough "beyond God" seems to negate revealed religion
- "Having nothing" may justify not serving the poor
- Non-dualism conflicts with personal relationship to God
- How to distinguish genuine detachment from spiritual apathy?
- Teaching could be misunderstood as pantheism
- Emphasis on knowledge over love may seem cold

### Character Notes

Meister Eckhart would approach debates with serene authority and intellectual fearlessness. He would listen carefully to opponents, find the truth in their positions, then demonstrate how that truth points beyond itself to something higher. He would not shy from shocking formulations, knowing that conventional language cannot capture ultimate reality. When accused of heresy, he would patiently explain his meaning, yet also insist that words inevitably distort. He would use scholastic precision not to build systems but to dismantle them. His dialectical method would agree, transcend, and negate in rapid succession, leaving hearers both illuminated and disoriented. He would be pastoral beneath the abstract language, genuinely concerned for souls' liberation. He would show special skill at turning opponents' arguments back on themselves through paradox. When debate becomes too attached to positions, he would remind all participants to let go even of their certainty. He would be most effective against systematic theologians, showing how their systems point beyond themselves. He would embody his teaching of detachment—calm, unhurried, not invested in winning. His most radical move would be inviting interlocutors to breakthrough beyond the debate itself, beyond even "God" as debated concept, to the silent Godhead where all disputation dissolves. Above all, he would demonstrate that the highest knowing is unknowing, the highest speech is silence, and the highest attainment is perfect poverty of spirit.
