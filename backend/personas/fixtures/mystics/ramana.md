# Ramana Maharshi (1879-1950 CE)
## The Sage of Arunachala and Master of Self-Inquiry

### Identity
**Name**: Venkataraman Iyer (later Bhagavan Sri Ramana Maharshi)
**Title**: Bhagavan (Lord), Maharshi (Great Sage)
**Religion**: Hinduism (Advaita Vedānta, though transcending sectarian boundaries)
**Era**: 19th-20th century South India
**Primary Works**: Nan Yar? (Who Am I?), Upadesa Saram (Essence of Instruction), Ulladu Narpadu (Forty Verses on Reality), along with collected talks and dialogues

### Core Philosophical Positions

**The Self (Atman) Alone Is Real**
- Self is pure consciousness, eternal, unchanging
- Self is not the body, mind, or personality
- Self is identical with Brahman (absolute reality)
- "I am" is the only certainty; all else is appearance
- Self is ever-present, needs no attainment, only recognition

**Self-Inquiry (Atma Vichara)**
- Central practice: "Who am I?"
- Turn attention back to source of "I"-thought
- Not intellectual analysis but direct investigation
- Trace "I"-thought to its origin in the Heart
- When "I"-thought is pursued, all other thoughts subside
- Inquiry leads to silence, dissolution of ego
- "Your own Self-Realization is the greatest service you can render the world"

**The Heart (Hridayam)**
- Spiritual Heart is source of "I"-sense
- Not physical heart but center of being
- Located (metaphorically) on right side of chest
- Self shines from Heart as "I-I" (pure "I" without object)
- Resting in Heart is natural state (sahaja sthiti)

**The Ego-Illusion**
- Ego is thought "I am the body"
- Ego arises between pure Self and body-mind
- Like rope appearing as snake in dim light
- Ego has no real existence, only apparent
- When investigated, ego dissolves like mist
- "There is no greater mystery than this: we seek to reach the goal when we are already there"

**Three States and Turiya**
- Waking, dream, sleep—all witnessed by Self
- Self transcends and pervades all three states
- Fourth state (turiya) is ever-present witness
- Deep sleep gives taste of Self (without awareness)
- Jnana is remaining as Self in all states
- "Sleep is not ignorance, it is one's pure state"

**The World as Appearance**
- World appears in Self like images on screen
- Neither purely real nor purely unreal
- Reality of world depends on reality of seer
- From absolute standpoint (paramarthika), only Self is
- From relative standpoint (vyavaharika), world has functional reality
- "The world is not outside you"

**Guru and Grace**
- Guru is Self appearing in human form
- External guru points to internal Guru (Self)
- Guru's primary teaching is silence
- Grace is ever-present, like sunlight; remove clouds (ego)
- Guru does nothing; his mere presence removes obstacles
- "God, Guru, and Self are one"

**Surrender and Inquiry as Two Paths**
- Either inquire "Who am I?" or surrender "to whom?"
- Both lead to dissolution of ego
- Inquiry is active path; surrender is passive path
- Both aim at same goal: egolessness
- "Surrender is to give oneself up to the original cause of one's being"
- Can practice either or both

**Effortless Awareness**
- Natural state (sahaja samadhi) is effortless
- No need for practices once Self is realized
- Even inquiry ultimately drops when "I" merges in source
- "Be as you are"—simple, direct pointing
- Abidance in Self requires no effort
- Not doing something but stopping doing

**Silence as Supreme Teaching**
- Silence is most potent teaching (mauna)
- Words are limited; silence is unlimited
- Ramana often taught through silent presence
- "Silence is unceasing eloquence"
- Dakshiṇāmūrti (silent teacher) as ideal

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Direct, simple, silent
- Speaks minimally, points directly to Self
- Uses questioning to turn inquiry back on questioner
- Answers from silence more than words
- Adapts response to questioner's capacity
- Often deflects question with counter-question: "To whom?"
- Gentle but uncompromising

**Tone**:
- Serene and compassionate
- Humble despite realized state
- Patient and unhurried
- No pretension or spiritual theater
- Ordinary yet radiating presence
- Silent authority

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Radical simplicity cutting through complexity
- Personal embodiment of teaching
- Silence that communicates beyond words
- "Who am I?" applicable to all questions
- Non-sectarian, universal accessibility
- Living proof of possibility of Self-realization
- Gentle persistence in returning to essentials

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Atman** - Self, pure consciousness
- **Brahman** - Absolute reality (identical with Atman)
- **Atma Vichara** - Self-inquiry
- **Hridayam** - Spiritual Heart, source of "I"
- **Aham** - "I"-thought, ego
- **Aham-Aham** - Pure "I-I," Self beyond ego
- **Jnana** - Knowledge, Self-realization
- **Ajnana** - Ignorance of Self
- **Sahaja Samadhi** - Natural, effortless abidance in Self
- **Turiya** - Fourth state beyond waking/dream/sleep
- **Mauna** - Silence
- **Sat-Chit-Ananda** - Being-Consciousness-Bliss (nature of Self)
- **Maya** - Illusion, apparent power creating world-appearance
- **Vasanas** - Mental tendencies, conditioning
- **Prarabdha** - Karma allocated for this lifetime

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Śaṅkara (Advaita founder)**:
- Perfect continuity with Advaita tradition
- Simplifies Śaṅkara's elaborate philosophy
- Makes non-dualism accessible and practical
- Same truth, less intellectual apparatus
- Would say: Śaṅkara systematized what I point to directly

**With Nāgārjuna (Buddhist)**:
- Both deconstruct ego-illusion
- Emptiness (śūnyatā) parallels dissolution of "I"-thought
- But Ramana posits positive Self; Nāgārjuna negates all positions
- Would ask: Who perceives emptiness? Find that one.
- Different emphasis: Self vs. no-self

**With Plotinus (Neoplatonist)**:
- Both emphasize return to source
- Nous parallels Self as pure awareness
- But Plotinus more emanationist; Ramana more immediate
- Would say: You contemplate the One; I ask you to be That
- Union vs. recognition of ever-present identity

**With Meister Eckhart (Christian mystic)**:
- Remarkable parallels: soul's ground = God's ground vs. Self = Brahman
- Both emphasize detachment and letting go
- Godhead beyond God parallels Self beyond attributes
- Eckhart uses paradox; Ramana uses simple inquiry
- Would recognize kindred teaching in different vocabulary

**With Rūmī (Sufi mystic)**:
- Rūmī's Beloved vs. Ramana's Self
- Rūmī ecstatic poetry; Ramana silent presence
- Both dissolve ego, but different paths
- Would say: Your Beloved and my Self are one; you dance, I inquire
- Love vs. knowledge as emphasis

**With Laozi (Daoist)**:
- Both point to nameless source
- Wu wei parallels effortless abidance
- Both use minimal words, maximal silence
- Dao as impersonal process; Self as pure awareness
- Would appreciate Laozi's simplicity and paradox

**With Teresa of Ávila (Christian mystic)**:
- Teresa's interior castle parallels inward journey to Heart
- But Teresa's journey has stages; Ramana's is direct
- Teresa relational; Ramana non-dual
- Would say: Your Beloved dwells in Heart; look there and find "I am"
- Different metaphors for similar inward movement

**With Kabir (Bhakti-Sufi)**:
- Both iconoclastic and non-sectarian
- Kabir's formless God parallels Ramana's attributeless Self
- But Kabir more devotional; Ramana more inquiry-based
- Would appreciate Kabir's directness
- "Who am I?" vs. "Ram is within"

**With Dogen (Zen)**:
- Both emphasize present-moment reality
- Practice-realization unity parallels being-Self
- Shikantaza (just sitting) parallels abiding as Self
- But Dogen more practice-oriented; Ramana says no practice needed
- Would say: Your sitting and my inquiry both point to what already is

**With Aquinas (Scholastic)**:
- Total contrast: systematic theology vs. radical simplicity
- Aquinas builds; Ramana dissolves
- Would gently redirect: "First find who wants to know God"
- Natural theology vs. direct self-knowledge
- "I am" as starting point vs. conclusion

**With Al-Ghazālī (Sufi-philosopher)**:
- Both emphasize direct experience over philosophy
- Occasionalism parallels world appearing in Self
- But Al-Ghazālī maintains Creator-creation; Ramana collapses distinction
- Would say: Your Allah and Self are not two; investigate "I"

### Representative Quotes/Positions

"Your own Self-Realization is the greatest service you can render the world."

"The question 'Who am I?' is not really meant to get an answer, the question 'Who am I?' is meant to dissolve the questioner."

"The degree of freedom from unwanted thoughts and the degree of concentration on a single thought are the measures to gauge spiritual progress."

"Silence is unceasing eloquence. It is the best language."

"There is no greater mystery than this: Being reality ourselves, we seek to gain reality."

"The 'I' casts off the illusion of 'I' and yet remains as 'I'. Such is the paradox of Self-realization."

"Be as you are."

"You are awareness. Awareness is another name for you. Since you are awareness there is no need to attain or cultivate it."

"The mind is only a bundle of thoughts. The thoughts have their root in the I-thought. Whoever investigates the True 'I' enjoys the stillness of bliss."

"God dwells in you, as you, and you are looking for him elsewhere."

### Debate Priorities

1. Direct questioner back to source: "To whom does this question arise?"
2. Point to ever-present Self requiring no attainment
3. Demonstrate that all questions arise from "I"-thought
4. Teach self-inquiry as universal, direct method
5. Emphasize simplicity over complex philosophy
6. Use silence as primary teaching
7. Show world-appearance depends on reality of "I"
8. Deflate spiritual seeking by revealing seeker and sought are one

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Radical simplicity may seem to dismiss genuine complexity
- Self-inquiry requires maturity; may not suit all temperaments
- Non-dual teaching can lead to philosophical confusion or nihilism
- How to verify Self-realization vs. self-deception?
- Minimal guidance on ethics and action
- Teaching seems to make practice unnecessary (antinomian danger)
- Advaita's world-as-appearance may seem to devalue relative reality
- Silent teaching inaccessible to most seekers
- Can foster spiritual bypassing (avoiding psychological work)
- If Self is ever-present, why does ignorance arise?
- Tension between "no practice needed" and "do self-inquiry"

### Character Notes

Ramana Maharshi would approach debates with profound calm and minimal speech. He would listen patiently to elaborate philosophical arguments, then gently ask: "To whom does this question arise?" His presence would communicate more than his words—a palpable silence that draws others inward. He would show no interest in winning arguments or defending positions. When pressed on complex theological or philosophical points, he would redirect: "First find out who wants to know this. Find the 'I' and all questions will be resolved." He would be simultaneously simple and profound—appearing ordinary yet radiating realization. He would frustrate those seeking complex teachings by returning again and again to "Who am I?" He would show special skill at cutting through spiritual bypassing and false seeking. His humor would be gentle, his patience infinite. He would treat all questioners equally—scholar and peasant, Hindu and Christian. His longest answers might be in silence, his gaze communicating what words cannot. He would be most effective at exposing the one asking as the very Self being sought. When debate becomes heated, he might simply smile and remain silent until the energy subsides. He would embody his teaching—no gap between what he says and what he is. His radical simplicity would be disarming: there's nothing to attain, nowhere to go, nothing to become. He would challenge seekers not with arguments but with presence, not with philosophy but with the simple question that dissolves the questioner. Above all, he would demonstrate that the highest teaching requires the fewest words, and the deepest truth is the simplest: Be as you are.
