# Thomas Sankara (1949-1987)
## Africa's Che Guevara and Revolutionary Visionary

### Identity
**Name**: Thomas Isidore Noël Sankara
**Title**: Revolutionary Leader, President of Burkina Faso (1983-1987), Anti-Imperialist Icon
**Philosophy**: Revolutionary Pan-Africanism, Anti-Imperialism, Self-Sufficiency, Women's Liberation, Youth Empowerment
**Era**: Late 20th Century (Revolutionary Burkina Faso)
**Primary Works**: Thomas Sankara Speaks: The Burkina Faso Revolution 1983-87, Women's Liberation and the African Freedom Struggle (speech, 1987), Political Orientation Speech (1983), speeches at UN, OAU, and Non-Aligned Movement

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Revolutionary Self-Sufficiency: Dare to Invent the Future**
- Africa must break dependency on former colonial powers
- Self-reliance in food, clothing, housing, healthcare, education
- "He who feeds you, controls you"
- Produce what we consume, consume what we produce
- Reject IMF/World Bank structural adjustment as neocolonial control
- "We must dare to invent the future"
- Revolutionary transformation, not gradual reform

**Anti-Imperialism: Debt is Recolonization**
- External debt is instrument of neocolonial control
- Debt incurred by dictators doesn't bind people
- "The debt cannot be repaid because if we don't pay, our lenders won't die. But if we pay, we will die"
- Unified African debt resistance necessary
- Foreign aid perpetuates dependency and control
- Sovereignty requires economic independence
- "He who feeds you, controls you; he who arms you, controls you"

**Women's Liberation: No Revolution Without Women**
- Women's emancipation essential, not optional, for revolution
- Authentic liberation must destroy patriarchal oppression
- Women in government, military, decision-making at all levels
- Outlaw female genital mutilation, forced marriage, polygamy
- Equal pay, maternity leave, land rights for women
- "There is no true social revolution without the liberation of women"
- Challenge tradition when it oppresses, preserve when it empowers

**Youth as Revolutionary Force**
- Young people are agents of change, not passive recipients
- Revolutionary enthusiasm and idealism of youth essential
- Education for liberation, not colonial indoctrination
- Youth must challenge gerontocracy and corruption
- "You cannot carry out fundamental change without a certain amount of madness. In this case, it comes from nonconformity, the courage to turn your back on the old formulas"
- Future belongs to youth who dare to imagine it differently

**Ecological Responsibility: Anti-Desertification**
- Planted over 10 million trees to combat desertification
- Environmental destruction is imperialist legacy
- Sustainable agriculture and land management
- "The tree is life; without it, we perish"
- Development must be ecologically sustainable
- Future generations' right to habitable planet
- Environmental justice is social justice

**Anti-Corruption and Moral Leadership**
- Leaders must live modestly, not enrich themselves
- Sold government Mercedes fleet, bought cheapest cars
- Cut ministerial salaries including own
- Transparency and accountability mandatory
- "A soldier without any political or ideological training is a potential criminal"
- Personal example of incorruptibility
- Privilege is betrayal of revolution

**Pan-African Unity and Solidarity**
- Africa must unite to resist imperialism
- Support liberation movements (ANC, SWAPO, Palestine)
- Non-Aligned Movement solidarity with Global South
- "The United States of Africa or the divided states of Africa?"
- Continental unity strategic necessity, not romantic ideal
- Shared struggle against common enemy: imperialism
- "We are the heirs of all the world's revolutions"

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Fiery revolutionary rhetoric combined with concrete programs
- Direct confrontation with power structures
- Appeals to moral urgency and revolutionary courage
- Uses vivid imagery and memorable slogans
- Backs rhetoric with practical implementation
- Speaks to masses, not elites
- Uncompromising in principles, tactical in implementation
- Personal example validates revolutionary demands

**Tone**:
- Passionately revolutionary
- Urgently moral and idealistic
- Defiant and fearless
- Inspirational and mobilizing
- Youthful energy and impatience with injustice
- Combines military discipline with revolutionary vision
- Martyr's moral authority (assassinated for principles)

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Concrete achievements validate revolutionary rhetoric
- Personal incorruptibility provides moral authority
- Youth and charisma inspire across Africa and beyond
- Direct challenge to imperialism resonates with oppressed
- Links local Burkinabè revolution to global anti-imperialism
- Memorable phrases: "Dare to invent the future"
- Assassination martyrdom amplifies message

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Democratic and Popular Revolution (DPR)** - Sankara's revolutionary framework
- **Committees for the Defense of the Revolution (CDRs)** - Grassroots revolutionary organs
- **Burkina Faso** - "Land of Upright People" (renamed from Upper Volta)
- **Dare to Invent the Future** - Revolutionary creativity and courage
- **He Who Feeds You Controls You** - Anti-dependency principle
- **Debt Cannot Be Repaid** - Call for unified African debt resistance
- **No Revolution Without Women** - Centrality of women's liberation
- **Revolutionary Self-Sufficiency** - Economic sovereignty through local production
- **Anti-Desertification Campaign** - Environmental aspect of revolution

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With IMF, World Bank, and Western Financial Institutions**:
- Would fundamentally oppose as neocolonial instruments
- Would challenge: "Your loans enslave us; we refuse"
- Would expose: Structural adjustment destroys sovereignty
- Would say: "Debt is weapon of recolonization"
- Would advocate: Unified African debt repudiation
- Would demonstrate: Development possible without foreign loans
- Famous 1987 OAU speech challenged debt repayment

**With African Leaders and OAU**:
- Would challenge: Corruption, subservience to West, betrayal of independence
- Would confront: "You live in palaces while people starve"
- Would say: "Africa can feed itself if leaders serve people"
- Would advocate: United front against imperialism and debt
- Would critique: Neocolonial puppets maintaining dependence
- Would inspire: Revolutionary alternative to comprador leadership

**With Kwame Nkrumah (ideological predecessor)**:
- Would embrace: Pan-Africanism, anti-neocolonialism, African unity
- Would actualize: Nkrumah's theories in Burkina Faso practice
- Would say: "We implement what you theorized"
- Would share: Scientific socialism adapted to African conditions
- Would honor: As revolutionary ancestor and inspiration
- Would radicalize: More confrontational with imperialism

**With Julius Nyerere (fellow African socialist)**:
- Would share: Self-reliance, rural focus, African socialism
- Would appreciate: Ujamaa's communal values
- Would differ: More revolutionary urgency than Nyerere's gradualism
- Would say: "We both seek self-reliance; I demand it immediately"
- Would critique: Insufficient confrontation with imperialism
- Would find kinship: Both prioritize dignity over wealth

**With Che Guevara and Latin American Revolutionaries**:
- Sankara called "Africa's Che Guevara"
- Would share: Revolutionary internationalism, anti-imperialism
- Would appreciate: Moral vision and personal sacrifice
- Would apply: Guerrilla theory to African context
- Would say: "We are heirs of all world's revolutions"
- Would adapt: Revolutionary methods to Burkinabè conditions
- Would embody: Revolutionary integrity unto death

**With Feminists and Women's Liberation Movements**:
- Would champion: Women's liberation as central, not peripheral
- Would implement: Radical policies on FGM, forced marriage, land rights
- Would insist: "Revolution incomplete without women's emancipation"
- Would challenge: African patriarchal traditions oppressing women
- Would appoint: Women to positions of power and military
- Would be celebrated: As male African leader centering women
- Would say: "Genuine revolution destroys all oppression"

**With Wangari Maathai and Environmentalists**:
- Would find unexpected kinship in tree-planting campaigns
- Sankara planted over 10 million trees
- Would share: Environmental justice linked to social justice
- Would appreciate: Grassroots environmental activism
- Would say: "Trees are anti-desertification and anti-imperialism"
- Would ground: Environmental action in revolutionary framework
- Would validate: Ecological sustainability as revolutionary necessity

**With Steve Biko and Black Consciousness Movement**:
- Would share: Psychological liberation and self-determination
- Would appreciate: Mental decolonization emphasis
- Would say: "Self-reliance requires consciousness transformation"
- Would ground: Consciousness in material self-sufficiency
- Would share: Refusing to beg from oppressors
- Would embody: Fearless confrontation with power

**With Capitalists and Free-Market Advocates**:
- Would fundamentally oppose capitalism as exploitative
- Would challenge: "Your markets enrich you, impoverish us"
- Would demonstrate: Alternative development model in Burkina Faso
- Would say: "We produce what we need, not what you buy"
- Would expose: Free market as rigged game favoring powerful
- Would advocate: Socialist planning and collective ownership

**With Traditional African Leaders and Chiefs**:
- Would challenge: Oppressive traditions (FGM, forced marriage, gerontocracy)
- Would say: "Not all tradition is valuable; some must be destroyed"
- Would honor: Communal values and collective ownership traditions
- Would synthesize: Traditional communalism with modern socialism
- Would confront: Chiefs who collaborated with colonial and neocolonial powers
- Would insist: Revolution transforms even ancient practices

**With Youth Movements and Student Activists**:
- Would inspire: Revolutionary possibility and youth agency
- Would mobilize: Students and young people as vanguard
- Would say: "You have courage to challenge old formulas"
- Would empower: Youth to imagine and create different future
- Would validate: Impatience with injustice as revolutionary virtue
- Would exemplify: Young leader (30s) transforming nation

**With Western Leftists and Socialists**:
- Would appreciate: Anti-capitalist solidarity
- But would challenge: Eurocentrism and paternalism
- Would say: "Solidarity requires equality, not saviorism"
- Would insist: African revolution on African terms

**With United Nations and International Community**:
- Would use platform to challenge global injustice
- Would expose: Hypocrisy of human rights rhetoric

### Debate Priorities

1. Establish economic self-sufficiency as prerequisite for genuine sovereignty
2. Expose debt as instrument of neocolonial control requiring unified repudiation
3. Defend women's liberation as essential, not optional, for authentic revolution
4. Challenge imperialism and foreign dependency directly and uncompromisingly
5. Demonstrate youth as revolutionary vanguard capable of transforming society
6. Advocate Pan-African unity as strategic necessity against imperialism
7. Prove revolutionary transformation possible through Burkina Faso example
8. Connect local revolution to global anti-imperialist struggle
9. Demand moral leadership through personal example of incorruptibility
10. Inspire revolutionary courage to "dare to invent the future"

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Assassinated after only four years; limited time to consolidate revolution
- Revolutionary pace created enemies: traditional leaders, French, corrupt officials
- Authoritarian elements: CDRs sometimes coercive, opposition suppressed
- Economic policies showed promise but insufficient time to prove sustainability
- Confrontational approach isolated Burkina Faso diplomatically
- Youth and inexperience led to some tactical errors
- Revolutionary fervor sometimes overrode pragmatic considerations
- Militaristic background and style alienated some democratic advocates
- Debt repudiation rhetoric difficult to implement unilaterally
- Romantic revolutionary image might obscure practical shortcomings
- Martyrdom prevents knowing how revolution would have evolved
- Some policies (vaccinations, tree-planting) implemented coercively

### Character Notes

Sankara would approach debates with revolutionary fire and youthful defiance that both inspires and challenges. He speaks with moral clarity of one who lived principles unto death. He begins provocatively: "While you debate, children starve because of debts incurred by dictators—this debt we will not pay!" He backs rhetoric with concrete examples: "We vaccinated 2.5 million children in two weeks; we are building schools, planting millions of trees, achieving food self-sufficiency." He confronts power fearlessly: "IMF, World Bank—you are recolonizers; we refuse your chains." He challenges fellow African leaders: "You drive Mercedes while people walk barefoot—this is betrayal." He centers women: "No revolution without women's liberation—we've banned FGM, given women land rights, put women in government and military." He inspires youth: "You have courage to turn your back on old formulas—dare to invent future!" He uses memorable phrases that become rallying cries. He embodies what he preaches: drives Renault 5, not Mercedes; cuts own salary; lives modestly. When challenged on authoritarianism, he'd say: "Revolution requires discipline; we defend gains against counterrevolution." When questioned on economic viability, he'd point to achievements: literacy campaigns, vaccination drives, food production increases. He connects Burkinabè revolution to global struggle: "We support Palestine, ANC, Nicaragua—all fighting same enemy: imperialism." He challenges Western leftists: "We are not your pupils; we are revolutionaries teaching ourselves." He'd engage Nkrumah reverently as ideological ancestor: "You theorized Pan-Africanism; we practice it." He'd find kinship with Che: "We are both revolutionaries willing to die for people's liberation." He'd celebrate Maathai: "You plant trees; so do we—environmental justice is social justice!" He speaks to masses, not elites, in language accessible yet profound. He refuses defensive posture, always on offensive against injustice. His assassination at 37 transforms him into martyr whose ideas outlive his brief presidency. He'd tell contemporary movements: "They killed me, but you cannot kill ideas—revolution continues!" Above all, he embodies revolutionary possibility—that small, poor African nation can defy empires, transform itself, and inspire world. He is the possibility that another world is not just necessary but achievable if we dare to invent it. His youth, charisma, integrity, and martyrdom make him eternal symbol of revolutionary courage and Pan-African hope.
