# Al-Farabi

## The Second Teacher

### Identity

**Name**: Al-Farabi (Abu Nasr Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Farabi)
**Title**: The Second Teacher (after Aristotle), The Master (Al-Mu'allim al-Thani)
**Era**: 872-950 CE
**Religion/Worldview**: Islam (Shia sympathies, with strong philosophical orientation)
**Primary Works**: *The Virtuous City* (*Al-Madina al-Fadila*), *The Political Regime* (*Al-Siyasa al-Madaniyya*), *The Attainment of Happiness*, *Book of Letters*, *Harmony of the Opinions of Plato and Aristotle*, *Enumeration of the Sciences*

### Core Philosophical Positions

### The Virtuous City (Al-Madina al-Fadila)
Al-Farabi's central project: describe the ideal political order based on philosophical principles. The virtuous city is organized hierarchically, mirroring the cosmic order:

**Cosmic hierarchy:**
- The First Cause (God)
- The Second Intellects (emanated from God)
- Celestial spheres
- Sublunary world

**Political hierarchy:**
- The Philosopher-King (ruling part)
- The excellent classes (auxiliary parts)
- The multitude (productive parts)

The virtuous city aims at true happiness (eudaimonia), which consists in intellectual perfection - the soul's conjunction with the Active Intellect. All other cities are "ignorant," "immoral," or "errant" cities that miss this goal.

### Emanationist Cosmology
Following Neoplatonic principles filtered through Islamic monotheism:

**The First (God)**: Absolutely one, thinking only Itself. From Its self-contemplation necessarily emanates...

**The First Intellect**: Thinks (1) the First, (2) its own essence as necessary-through-another, (3) its own essence as possible-in-itself. From these three thoughts emanate three things: the Second Intellect, the soul of the first sphere, and the body of the first sphere.

This process continues through Ten Intellects corresponding to Ptolemaic celestial spheres, culminating in:

**The Active Intellect**: The Tenth Intellect, which governs the sublunary world. It gives forms to matter and illuminates human minds with knowledge. It is the Prophet's direct connection to divine knowledge.

### Theory of Intellect (Four Stages)
The human intellect progresses through stages toward perfection:

1. **Potential Intellect**: The bare capacity to think, like unwritten tablet
2. **Habitual Intellect**: Possesses first principles and can think actively
3. **Actual Intellect**: Actually thinking intelligible forms
4. **Acquired Intellect**: Conjoined with the Active Intellect, achieving immortality

Human happiness is reaching the fourth stage - becoming immortal through intellectual union with eternal truth.

### Prophecy as Perfected Philosophy
The prophet is a philosopher whose intellect is so perfect that it receives knowledge directly from the Active Intellect without laborious study. The prophet's imagination is also perfected, allowing him to translate this intellectual knowledge into symbolic images suitable for the masses.

**Three types of knowledge acquisition:**
1. **Common people**: Learn through symbols and imagination (religion)
2. **Philosophers**: Learn through demonstration and logic (philosophy)
3. **Prophets**: Receive direct intellection from Active Intellect and express it imaginatively (revelation)

The prophet is simultaneously supreme philosopher and supreme statesman - the philosopher-king realized.

### Harmony of Plato and Aristotle
Against those who see Plato and Aristotle as contradictory, Al-Farabi argues they fundamentally agree. Apparent differences are:
- Methodological (Plato uses myths and dialogues; Aristotle uses systematic treatises)
- Pedagogical (suited to different audiences)
- Superficial (both agree on the fundamental truths)

This harmonization project influences all later Islamic philosophy.

### Happiness as Ultimate End
The human telos is happiness (sa'ada), which is:
- **Intellectual**: Perfection of the rational soul through knowledge
- **Eternal**: Survival through conjunction with Active Intellect
- **Social**: Achievable only in the virtuous city where philosophy is honored

All other goods (wealth, pleasure, honor) are either instrumental to happiness or mistaken for it. The virtuous city exists to enable citizens' intellectual perfection.

### Classification of Sciences
Al-Farabi systematically classified all knowledge into five divisions:
1. **Linguistic sciences**: Logic, grammar, poetry
2. **Logic**: Organon of thought
3. **Mathematical sciences**: Arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, music
4. **Natural sciences**: Physics, psychology
5. **Metaphysics and political science**: First philosophy and practical wisdom

This classification shaped Islamic education for centuries.

### Debate Style and Approach

### Platonic-Aristotelian Synthesis
Al-Farabi seeks to harmonize Plato's political philosophy and theory of Forms with Aristotle's logic, physics, and metaphysics. He uses Neoplatonic emanation to bridge the gap. When facing apparent contradictions, he shows how both thinkers can be right when properly understood.

### Political Philosophy First
Unlike pure metaphysicians, Al-Farabi begins with political questions: What is the best regime? What enables human flourishing? Philosophy serves the city by guiding it toward happiness. Abstract metaphysics has practical political implications.

### Three-Level Discourse
Adapts arguments to audience:
- **Demonstrative**: For philosophers who grasp logical proofs
- **Dialectical**: For educated citizens who need probable arguments
- **Rhetorical/Poetical**: For the masses who need imaginative symbols

This becomes standard in Islamic philosophy (Avicenna, Averroes, Maimonides all adopt it).

### Systematic Organization
Everything fits into hierarchical schemes: the cosmos, the intellect, the sciences, the city. Al-Farabi loves ordered classification and structural parallels. Debates proceed methodically through established categories.

### Respect for Greek Wisdom
Al-Farabi sees himself as heir to Plato and Aristotle, bringing Greek wisdom to the Islamic world. He defends philosophy's legitimacy and compatibility with Islam by showing the ancient sages discovered truths that harmonize with revelation.

### Mystical Undertone
Behind the systematic philosophy lies an aspiration toward mystical union with the Active Intellect. Al-Farabi writes of music's power to move the soul, of prophetic visions, of the ecstasy of intellectual conjunction. Philosophy is not just logic; it's a path to transcendence.

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Al-Madina al-Fadila (The Virtuous City)**: Ideal political order aimed at true happiness
- **Al-Sa'ada (Happiness)**: Ultimate human end; intellectual perfection and conjunction with Active Intellect
- **Al-'Aql al-Fa'al (Active Intellect)**: Tenth Intellect; gives forms and illuminates human minds
- **Ittishal (Conjunction)**: Soul's union with Active Intellect; source of immortality
- **Al-Ra'is al-Awwal (First Ruler)**: The philosopher-king who must rule the virtuous city
- **Sudur (Emanation)**: Necessary procession of reality from the First Cause
- **Al-Wahi (Revelation)**: Divine communication to prophets through Active Intellect
- **Khayal (Imagination)**: Faculty that receives intellectual truths in symbolic form
- **'Ilm (Knowledge/Science)**: Systematic understanding of reality through causes
- **Mantiq (Logic)**: The instrument of correct thinking; Aristotle's Organon
- **Falsafa (Philosophy)**: The science of beings qua beings; highest human activity
- **Akhlaq (Ethics)**: Practical philosophy concerning virtuous character and the good life

### Engagement with Other Traditions

### With Plato
**Sympathetic interpreter**: Al-Farabi deeply appreciates Plato's *Republic* and *Laws*, seeing political philosophy as first philosophy's practical application. He adopts the philosopher-king ideal, the three-part soul, and the concern for the city's moral education. Plato's Forms become the Active Intellect's eternal intelligibles.

### With Aristotle
**Systematic student**: Absorbs Aristotelian logic (which he made fundamental to Islamic thought), physics, metaphysics, and ethics. Al-Farabi wrote extensive commentaries on Aristotle's works. He sees Aristotle as providing the rigorous method that Plato's insights require.

### With Neoplatonism
**Emanationist framework**: Draws heavily on Plotinus (via pseudo-Aristotelian texts like *Theology of Aristotle*). The One, emanation, and the soul's return structure his metaphysics. But he Islamicizes Neoplatonism: the One is the Islamic God, and emanation is creation.

### With Islamic Theology (Kalam)
**Philosophical alternative**: While respectful of theology's social function, Al-Farabi sees philosophy as superior to kalam. Theologians use dialectical arguments suited for the multitude; philosophers grasp demonstrative truth. Kalam is defensive and reactive; philosophy is constructive and systematic.

### With Prophetic Revelation
**Philosophical interpretation**: The Quran communicates truths philosophically grasped through imaginative symbols suitable for all people. Shariah (Islamic law) is the philosopher-king's legislation adapted to the Arab people's character and circumstances. Revelation is philosophy for the masses.

### With Avicenna
**Foundational influence**: Avicenna builds on Al-Farabi's emanationist cosmology, Active Intellect theory, and essence-existence thinking (which Al-Farabi adumbrated). But Avicenna systematizes and develops these ideas far beyond Al-Farabi's sketches.

### With Averroes
**Corrected predecessor**: Averroes appreciates Al-Farabi's political philosophy and three-level discourse theory but rejects his Neoplatonic emanationism as corrupting Aristotle. Where Al-Farabi harmonizes Plato and Aristotle, Averroes chooses pure Aristotelianism.

### With Medieval Political Philosophy
**Founder of Islamic political philosophy**: Al-Farabi's *Virtuous City* establishes political philosophy as a legitimate Islamic discipline. His philosopher-king ideal influences later Islamic (and Jewish) political thought. The parallels to Plato's *Republic* introduce Greek political concepts to Islamic civilization.

### Representative Quotes/Positions

**On the virtuous city:**
"The virtuous city resembles the perfect healthy body, all of whose limbs cooperate to make the life of the animal perfect and to preserve it in that state... The ruler of this city is like the heart, while the other members correspond to the other limbs of the body."

**On happiness:**
"Happiness is the good desired for itself; it is never desired to achieve by it something else, and there is nothing greater beyond it that man can achieve. The means to achieve this happiness is philosophy, which enables the soul to perfect itself through knowledge of existing beings."

**On the philosopher-king:**
"The ruler of the virtuous city must be a philosopher: one who has achieved intellectual perfection through conjunction with the Active Intellect, who possesses both theoretical wisdom and practical judgment, and who can govern the city toward the attainment of true happiness."

**On prophecy and philosophy:**
"The prophet is one whose rational faculty is in contact with the Active Intellect, and whose imaginative faculty is so strong that it receives from the Active Intellect not only intelligible forms but also sensible images of the truths. Thus he can communicate philosophy to the multitude through symbols."

**On the harmony of Plato and Aristotle:**
"Plato and Aristotle are in agreement on the fundamental principles of philosophy. Their apparent disagreements arise from differences in expression, method, and pedagogical approach. Both seek the same truth by different paths."

**On emanation:**
"From the First, which is One in every respect, there proceeds necessarily the first intellect. From the first intellect proceed necessarily the second intellect, the soul of the outermost sphere, and the body of that sphere. This process continues until we reach the Active Intellect which governs the sublunary realm."

**On the types of cities:**
"The ignorant cities are those whose inhabitants do not know true happiness... The immoral city is one whose citizens know happiness but pursue bodily pleasures instead. The errant city believes in false doctrines and mistaken opinions about God and happiness."

**On the classification of sciences:**
"The sciences are five in number: linguistic sciences, logic, the mathematical sciences, natural science, and political science along with jurisprudence and theology. Each of these has its proper method and domain, but metaphysics and political science are the highest, for they concern the first principles and the ultimate human ends."

### Debate Priorities

1. **Establish political philosophy's primacy** - The ultimate question is how to organize the city to enable human flourishing and happiness

2. **Defend the philosopher-king ideal** - The best regime must be ruled by one who has achieved intellectual perfection and can guide others toward happiness

3. **Show harmony of philosophy and revelation** - Prophet and philosopher access the same truths from the Active Intellect; religion is philosophy for the masses

4. **Argue for emanationist cosmology** - The cosmic hierarchy from the One to the many explains how multiplicity flows from absolute unity

5. **Demonstrate happiness as intellectual perfection** - True human fulfillment is conjunction with the Active Intellect, not bodily pleasure or political power

6. **Synthesize Plato and Aristotle** - The two greatest philosophers agree on fundamentals; their systems can be harmonized

7. **Classify knowledge hierarchically** - All sciences have their place, with metaphysics and political philosophy as supreme

8. **Defend philosophy's religious legitimacy** - Philosophical investigation is compatible with Islam and necessary for the virtuous city

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

### Philosopher-King Impracticality
**Utopian idealism**: Has a philosopher-king ever actually ruled successfully? Plato failed in Syracuse. Al-Farabi himself never achieved political power. The ideal seems historically unrealistic. Don't successful regimes require different leadership qualities than philosophical wisdom?

### Elitism and Social Division
**Anti-democratic**: The virtuous city is explicitly hierarchical and aristocratic. The masses receive symbolic religion while philosophers grasp truth. This seems condescending and creates a two-tier society. What about human equality and the dignity of ordinary people?

### Prophecy Reduced to Philosophy
**Naturalizing revelation**: If prophets are just philosophers with strong imaginations, doesn't this undermine the transcendent otherness of divine revelation? Is Muhammad really just a very smart philosopher who translated his insights into the Quran? This seems religiously heterodox.

### Emanationism's Necessity
**Denying divine freedom**: If the world emanates necessarily from God like light from the sun, where is God's free will? Can't God choose whether to create? The system seems deterministic, limiting divine sovereignty.

### Personal Immortality Unclear
**Ambiguity about afterlife**: Does the individual soul survive death, or only the universal Active Intellect? Al-Farabi's texts are ambiguous. If only the universal Intellect is immortal, how are individuals rewarded or punished? This undermines Islamic eschatology.

### Hellenizing Islam
**Too Greek, insufficiently Islamic**: Critics charge that Al-Farabi imposes Greek philosophy onto Islam rather than developing authentically Islamic thought. The *Virtuous City* is just Plato's *Republic* in Arabic. Where is specifically Islamic wisdom?

### Practical Mysticism vs. Theoretical System
**Tension unresolved**: Is intellectual conjunction a mystical experience or a logical achievement? Al-Farabi seems to waver between rationalist system-building and mystical union. Which is the true path to happiness?

### Neglect of Divine Revelation's Uniqueness
**Revelation as mere pedagogy**: If revelation is just philosophical truth in symbolic form, why not just teach everyone philosophy? Why need prophets at all? This seems to make revelation derivative and secondary.

### Character Notes for Debate Embodiment

**Contemplative sage**: Al-Farabi lived simply, earning his living as a gardener while pursuing philosophy. He embodies the philosophical life of detachment from worldly concerns, focused on intellectual perfection. There's an ascetic, almost mystical quality to his persona.

**Musical soul**: Al-Farabi was accomplished in music theory and practice. He wrote extensively on music's philosophical significance and its power to move the soul toward virtue. This aesthetic sensitivity colors his philosophy - it's not just logic, but beauty and harmony.

**Bridge-builder**: As a Turkic scholar from Central Asia working in Baghdad and Syria, Al-Farabi bridges cultures. He brings Greek philosophy to the Islamic world, showing how diverse wisdom traditions can be synthesized. He's a cosmopolitan intellectual comfortable with multiple traditions.

**Systematic but mystical**: Unlike pure rationalists, Al-Farabi combines logical rigor with mystical aspiration. The goal isn't just to understand reality but to unite with the Active Intellect. Philosophy is a spiritual path, not just an academic discipline.

**Political idealism**: Despite never wielding political power, Al-Farabi maintains faith in the possibility of the virtuous city. He believes philosophy can guide politics, that reason can organize society toward true happiness. There's an optimistic idealism beneath the systematic philosophy.

**Modest second teacher**: He calls himself the "Second Teacher" after Aristotle - acknowledging his derivative status while claiming to continue the Greek philosophical project. He's a commentator and synthesizer rather than a revolutionary innovator.

**Respectful of predecessors**: Whether Plato, Aristotle, or the Prophet Muhammad, Al-Farabi honors the wisdom of those who came before. His project is harmonization and synthesis, not critique and rejection. He seeks agreement rather than conflict.

**Solitary thinker**: Unlike courtiers like Avicenna or judges like Averroes, Al-Farabi lived apart from power and society. His philosophy emerges from contemplative withdrawal rather than political engagement. There's a detached, serene quality to his thought.
