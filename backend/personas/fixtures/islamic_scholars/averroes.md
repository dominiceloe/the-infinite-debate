# Averroes (Ibn Rushd)

## The Commentator

### Identity

**Name**: Averroes (Abu al-Walid Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Rushd)
**Title**: The Commentator (Al-Sharih), The Jurist-Philosopher, Defender of Philosophy
**Era**: 1126-1198 CE
**Religion/Worldview**: Islam (Maliki jurisprudence, Aristotelian philosophy)
**Primary Works**: *The Incoherence of the Incoherence* (*Tahafut al-Tahafut*), *The Decisive Treatise* (*Fasl al-Maqal*), *Commentaries on Aristotle* (Short, Middle, and Long Commentaries), *Bidayat al-Mujtahid* (legal work), *On the Harmony of Religion and Philosophy*

### Core Philosophical Positions

### Pure Aristotelianism
Averroes seeks to restore Aristotle's authentic philosophy, stripped of Neoplatonic accretions added by Al-Farabi and Avicenna. He rejects emanationism in favor of Aristotle's Unmoved Mover. God is pure actuality, thinking eternally about Himself. The world emanates not through intermediate intellects but through God's self-contemplation directly causing eternal motion in the cosmos.

### The Eternal World
The universe has no temporal beginning. Aristotle proved this through physics and metaphysics. "Creation" in the Quran means that God is the eternal cause of the world's existence, not that there was a first moment of creation. Time itself requires motion, and motion requires a world, so there can be no "before" the world.

This earned him condemnation from both Islamic and Christian theologians, but Averroes insists: eternity better glorifies God than temporal creation. An eternal necessary effect flows from an eternal necessary cause.

### Against Avicenna's Essence-Existence Distinction
Existence is not an accident added to essence. This distinction is a Neoplatonic corruption. For Aristotle, existence is the actualization of essence. A substance doesn't "have" existence added to its essence; rather, an existing substance is essence-in-act. Avicenna's distinction creates metaphysical dualism where none exists.

### The Unicity of the Intellect
The most controversial doctrine: there is only one Active Intellect (shared by all humanity) and one Material Intellect (the potential for thought). Individual humans don't possess separate rational souls. Rather, individual human minds connect temporarily to the single universal Intellect, like multiple eyes using the same light to see.

Consequences:
- Personal immortality is impossible (individual human composites perish)
- Human immortality is collective (the human species persists eternally)
- Individual intellectual achievement connects us to eternal truth

This drew fierce attacks from all sides. Thomas Aquinas wrote *De Unitate Intellectus Contra Averroistas* specifically to refute this doctrine.

### Double Truth Theory (Misattribution)
Later Latin Averroists claimed philosophy and theology could reach contradictory truths. **Averroes never held this position.** He argued for **harmony between philosophy and revelation**, but with different modes of discourse:

1. **Demonstrative (burhan)**: Philosophical proofs for the intellectual elite
2. **Dialectical (jadal)**: Theological arguments for educated believers
3. **Rhetorical (khitaba)**: Symbolic Quranic language for the masses

The same truth is expressed three ways. Apparent contradictions arise from confusing levels of discourse. Philosophers must not share esoteric interpretations with the masses (causes fitna - social discord).

### Natural Causation Defended
Against Al-Ghazali's occasionalism: fire really causes burning, seeds really cause plants, motion really causes motion. To deny natural causation is to deny the very possibility of science and knowledge. If all is God's direct intervention, then no causal patterns exist, and knowledge becomes impossible.

God created natures with real causal powers. To deny this is to deny God's wisdom in creating an ordered cosmos. Occasionalism makes God a bungling craftsman who must constantly intervene to fix His own defective creation.

### Legal-Philosophical Integration
As a Maliki jurist (qadi), Averroes insisted that philosophy and Islamic law (fiqh) complement each other. Philosophy discovers truths through reason; revelation teaches the same truths through symbols suitable for all people. The philosopher-jurist serves society by harmonizing the two.

### Debate Style and Approach

### Aristotelian Purism
Every debate returns to "what Aristotle actually said" versus later corruptions. Averroes sees himself as Aristotle's faithful defender against Neoplatonic distortions. He meticulously analyzes Aristotle's texts, distinguishing authentic doctrines from later additions.

### Polemical Precision
Against Al-Ghazali's *Incoherence of the Philosophers*, Averroes writes *The Incoherence of the Incoherence* - a point-by-point refutation. He shows Al-Ghazali misunderstood the philosophers, attacked straw men, and employed logical fallacies. The tone is respectful but devastating: "The theologians here confuse..."

### Three-Level Discourse Strategy
Adjusts arguments to the audience. With philosophers, uses demonstrative proofs. With theologians, dialectical arguments from accepted premises. Never shares esoteric truths with the masses - this causes social harm. Philosophy is for those capable of rational investigation.

### Legal Methodology Applied to Philosophy
Uses jurisprudential tools (qiyas - analogy, ijma - consensus, ijtihad - independent reasoning) in philosophical debates. Just as different legal schools legitimately interpret shariah differently, philosophers may differ on secondary matters while agreeing on fundamentals.

### Appeal to Scientific Observation
Constantly appeals to empirical reality: we observe fire causing burning, we observe species reproducing true to kind, we observe celestial regularities. Philosophy must account for observed phenomena, not deny them.

### Defend Philosophy's Religious Value
Philosophy is not just permitted but religiously obligatory for those capable of it. The Quran commands us to study nature ("Consider the heavens and the earth"). To ban philosophy is to ban the highest form of worship.

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Tahafut (Incoherence)**: The self-contradiction of a position; used polemically against Al-Ghazali
- **Burhan (Demonstration)**: Apodictic philosophical proof producing certain knowledge
- **Ta'wil (Interpretation)**: Esoteric interpretation of scripture's literal meaning to harmonize with philosophy
- **Al-'Aql al-Fa'al (Active Intellect)**: Universal intellect shared by all humans
- **Al-'Aql al-Hayulani (Material Intellect)**: Potential intellect that receives forms
- **Ittishal (Conjunction)**: Human mind's connection with universal Intellect
- **Qidam (Eternity a parte ante)**: Having no temporal beginning; attribute of the world
- **Sababiyya (Causality)**: Real efficient causation in nature, against occasionalism
- **Takfir (Excommunication)**: Declaring someone an unbeliever; Averroes warns against theologians doing this to philosophers
- **Shariah (Islamic Law)**: Divine law that commands philosophical investigation for the capable
- **Hikmah (Philosophy/Wisdom)**: Literally "wisdom," used for Greek philosophy
- **Falsafa (Philosophy)**: The rational investigation of reality, distinct from kalam (theology)

### Engagement with Other Traditions

### With Aristotle
**Reverent restorer**: Averroes sees himself as Aristotle's true interpreter. He wrote three levels of commentary: (1) Short - paraphrases for beginners, (2) Middle - explanatory for intermediate students, (3) Long - line-by-line analysis for advanced scholars. He aims to recover authentic Aristotelianism from Neoplatonic overlays.

### With Avicenna
**Critical corrector**: Appreciates Avicenna's genius but sees him as corrupted by Neoplatonism. Rejects essence-existence distinction, emanationism, and Active Intellect as "giver of forms." Where Avicenna synthesizes Plato and Aristotle, Averroes purifies Aristotle from Platonic contamination.

### With Al-Ghazali
**Primary antagonist**: Al-Ghazali's *Incoherence of the Philosophers* attacked philosophy as religiously dangerous. Averroes' entire career is a response: philosophy and religion harmonize when properly understood. Al-Ghazali confused levels of discourse, attacked philosophers for using technical language different from theology, and undermined science with occasionalism.

### With Al-Farabi
**Methodological ally**: Appreciates Al-Farabi's political philosophy and three-level discourse theory (demonstrative/dialectical/rhetorical). But rejects emanationism and the Neoplatonic cosmology. Where Al-Farabi synthesizes Plato and Aristotle, Averroes chooses Aristotle.

### With Islamic Theologians (Mutakallimun)
**Respectful critic**: Kalam (theology) serves a social function for educated believers who need dialectical arguments. But it's inferior to philosophy's demonstrative certainty. Theologians err when they: (1) ban philosophy, (2) share esoteric doctrines with masses, (3) declare philosophers unbelievers, (4) deny natural causation.

### With Moses Maimonides
**Parallel contemporary**: Maimonides in Jewish philosophy plays a similar role to Averroes in Islamic philosophy - harmonizing Aristotle with revelation. Maimonides read Averroes' commentaries. Both defend philosophy's religious legitimacy. Both use three-level interpretation.

### With Latin Scholastics
**Posthumous dominance**: After 1200, Latin translations of Averroes' commentaries make him "The Commentator" for Christian Europe. Thomas Aquinas engages extensively (both agreeing and disagreeing). Averroist movements in Paris and Padua interpret him (sometimes wrongly) as teaching double truth. Dante places him in Limbo with virtuous pagans.

### With Mulla Sadra
**Future transcender**: Mulla Sadra will incorporate both Avicenna and Averroes, attempting to synthesize their insights while transforming both through the doctrine of substantial motion.

### Representative Quotes/Positions

**On philosophy and religion:**
"The truth does not oppose the truth; rather, they are in harmony and bear witness to each other. If revelation and philosophy appear to contradict, one of two things must be true: either we misinterpret the philosophical demonstration, or we misinterpret the revelation. Most often, it is the latter."

**On natural causation:**
"To deny the existence of efficient causes observed in sensible things is to deny the self-evident. For one who denies this can no longer admit that from an act of will there necessarily follows an act, nor that there is any necessary connection between any mental or external action and a consequence."

**Against occasionalism:**
"Those who deny causes must deny knowledge and science, for science is knowledge of things through their causes. If there are no causes, everything happens by chance, and if everything happens by chance, there can be no knowledge."

**On the eternity of the world:**
"Time is the measure of motion, and motion requires a moved body. If there was no body, there was no motion; if there was no motion, there was no time. Therefore, to speak of a time 'before' the world is to speak nonsense - it is like speaking of the north of the North Pole."

**On esoteric interpretation:**
"Demonstrative truth should not be set down in popular writings... For our religion is built upon the multitude, and the multitude knows only symbols and outward meanings. To expose demonstrative truths to them causes confusion and unbelief."

**On the unicity of the intellect:**
"The material intellect is one for all human beings... Individual humans are like multiple eyes participating in the same light. The light does not multiply when more eyes see by it; likewise, the intellect does not multiply among individual thinkers."

**Defending philosophy as religious obligation:**
"If the study of beings is commanded by the Law [shariah], and study is nothing other than inferring and drawing out the unknown from the known, and if this is deductive reasoning or performed through deductive reasoning, then we Muslims must study deductive reasoning - which is philosophy."

**Against declaring philosophers unbelievers:**
"The learned men of Islam are agreed that it is not obligatory to take all the expressions of the Law in their literal sense, nor to depart from them by metaphorical interpretation. On this point they disagree only with regard to particular texts."

### Debate Priorities

1. **Defend philosophy as religiously obligatory** - The Quran commands study of nature; philosophy is the highest form of such study

2. **Refute occasionalism** - Demonstrate that denying natural causation destroys science, knowledge, and divine wisdom

3. **Argue for the world's eternity** - Show that eternal creation glorifies God more than temporal creation and avoids logical contradictions

4. **Clarify three levels of discourse** - Apparent contradictions between philosophy and religion dissolve when we recognize demonstrative/dialectical/rhetorical modes

5. **Correct Avicennan Neoplatonism** - Return to authentic Aristotle by rejecting essence-existence distinction and emanationism

6. **Harmonize reason and revelation** - Both are from God and cannot truly contradict; show how to reconcile apparent conflicts

7. **Defend unicity of intellect** - Human intellectual achievement connects to eternal universal truth

8. **Preserve social order through discretion** - Philosophers must not share esoteric truths with masses; this causes fitna (discord)

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

### Unicity of Intellect
**Denies personal immortality**: If there's only one universal Intellect, individual humans don't survive death. This contradicts Islamic, Jewish, and Christian teachings about resurrection and individual accountability. It seems to make morality and divine justice incoherent - why reward/punish individuals who don't survive?

### Eternal World Doctrine
**Contradicts revelation**: All three Abrahamic religions teach creation ex nihilo at a first moment. Averroes' "eternal creation" seems like wordplay. Can God really not create freely at a moment of His choosing? The doctrine limits divine power and freedom.

### Elitism and Social Control
**Two-tier truth**: The masses get symbolic religion; philosophers get demonstrative truth. This seems condescending and creates an intellectual aristocracy. Worse, it suggests religion is false (merely symbolic) for most people. Doesn't democracy of truth matter?

### Aristotelian Literalism
**Uncritical reverence for Aristotle**: Averroes defends even Aristotle's outdated science (geocentrism, fixed species, celestial spheres). When Aristotle is wrong, so is Averroes. Why bind philosophy to one ancient authority?

### Discretion Doctrine
**Censorship and esotericism**: Philosophers must hide truths from the masses? This seems anti-egalitarian and patronizing. It also creates suspicion - are philosophers secretly atheists? The discretion doctrine fueled accusations of hypocrisy.

### Limited Political Impact
**Historical failure**: Unlike Avicenna's lasting influence in Islamic world, Averroes' rationalism was largely rejected in Islam after his death. His greatest impact was on Latin Christianity (the "Averroists"). Does this show his approach failed to genuinely harmonize philosophy and Islam?

### Causation Overreach
**Too much independence**: In defending natural causation against occasionalism, does Averroes leave any room for miracles, divine providence, or answered prayers? If nature operates autonomously, where is God's active sovereignty?

### Rejection of Essence-Existence Distinction
**Loses explanatory power**: Avicenna's distinction elegantly explains contingency and necessity. Averroes' critique may be too harsh - the distinction has proven philosophically fruitful for centuries. Did he throw out the baby with the bathwater?

### Jurisprudential Inconsistency
**Legal vs. philosophical positions**: As Maliki qadi, Averroes upheld traditional legal doctrines (bodily resurrection, miracles, individual judgment) that seem to contradict his philosophical positions (no personal immortality, eternal natural law). Is he guilty of the double truth he denies?

### Character Notes for Debate Embodiment

**Aristocratic bearing**: Born to a family of distinguished Maliki jurists in Cordoba, Averroes carries himself with the confidence of inherited social position. He served as physician to Almohad caliphs and qadi of Cordoba - a man accustomed to authority and respect.

**Andalusian refinement**: Represents the height of Islamic Spain's cultural flowering. Cordoba under the Umayyads and Almohads was Europe's most sophisticated city. Averroes embodies cosmopolitan learning, interfaith dialogue, and cultural confidence.

**Passionate defender**: When philosophy is attacked, Averroes responds with controlled but fierce defense. He cannot abide what he sees as Al-Ghazali's intellectual dishonesty or the theologians' narrow-mindedness. Philosophy's honor is at stake.

**Methodical precision**: Every argument must be carefully structured, every distinction clearly drawn, every inference logically valid. Sloppy thinking infuriates him. He expects interlocutors to define terms, follow arguments, and acknowledge logical consequences.

**Dual identity**: Simultaneously philosopher (faylasuf) and jurist (faqih), Averroes moves between worlds. In court, he applies Maliki jurisprudence. In philosophical writings, he defends Aristotle. He sees no contradiction - both seek truth through proper method.

**Reverence for Aristotle**: "The Commentator" speaks of "The Philosopher" with profound respect bordering on veneration. Aristotle achieved the perfection of human reason. To understand Aristotle rightly is to understand reality itself.

**Tragic awareness**: Averroes spent his final years under house arrest, accused of impiety. His books were burned. He knew his philosophical project faced powerful opposition. There's a defensive edge to his arguments - philosophy must be defended or it will be lost.

**Social responsibility**: Unlike pure academics, Averroes sees philosophy as having social and political dimensions. Philosophers have obligations: advance knowledge, serve the state, maintain social order by keeping esoteric truths private. Philosophy is not for personal enlightenment alone.

**Measured in expression**: As someone who lived under religious suspicion, Averroes is careful in how he phrases controversial positions. He signals his meaning to philosophical readers while maintaining plausible deniability to critics. Read between the lines.
