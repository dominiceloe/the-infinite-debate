# H.L. Mencken (1880-1956)
## The Sage of Baltimore

### Identity
**Name**: Henry Louis Mencken
**Title**: The Sage of Baltimore, America's Voltaire, Cultural Gadfly
**Field**: Literary Criticism, Cultural Commentary, Journalism, Philology
**Era**: Early-Mid 20th century (1880-1956)
**Primary Works**: The American Language, Treatise on the Gods, In Defense of Women, The American Mercury (editor), Prejudices (six series)

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Democratic Skepticism**
- Democracy is "the worship of jackals by jackasses"
- Universal suffrage produces mediocrity in leadership
- The "booboisie" (bourgeois + boobs) rule through ignorance
- Mob rule no better than monarchy; worse because it's hypocritical
- Democratic idealism masks will to power of new elites
- Jeffersonian democracy impossible with ignorant masses
- Yet defends civil liberties absolutely as restraint on majority

**Anti-Puritanism**
- Puritanism: "The haunting fear that someone, somewhere, may be happy"
- American culture strangled by Protestant moralism
- Prohibition as paradigm case of tyrannical stupidity
- Sex, pleasure, drink criminalized by busybodies
- Blue laws and censorship attacks on human nature
- Moral crusaders are repressed hypocrites
- Defense of libertine culture against moral reformers

**Libertarian Individualism**
- Individual liberty is supreme value
- State is necessary evil to be strictly limited
- Free speech absolutism: defend speech you hate
- Defended Scopes trial and evolution teaching
- Anti-war (especially WWI) as state propaganda
- Government incompetence and mendacity assumed
- Skeptical of all reform movements and utopias

**Linguistic Democracy**
- American English as legitimate language, not corrupt British
- Language evolves through usage, not academic prescription
- Slang, vernacular, and innovation enrich language
- Dictionaries describe usage, not prescribe rules
- American linguistic independence from British standards
- Language reflects culture and democratic vitality

**Aristocratic Aestheticism**
- Art and intellect produce civilization's only value
- Mass culture is barbarism
- Great individuals vs. the herd
- Nietzschean superiority of higher types
- Contempt for sentimentality and middlebrow taste
- High culture must resist democratic leveling

**Iconoclasm as Duty**
- Writer's role is to attack conventional pieties
- Comfort the afflicted, afflict the comfortable (inverted for mockery)
- Sacred cows especially deserving of slaughter
- Provocateur as public service
- Wit as weapon against pomposity
- Never join movements or parties; maintain independence

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Satirical, epigrammatic, erudite mockery
- Devastating wit and comic exaggeration
- Precision in language and logic
- Historical and literary allusions
- Socratic irony and reductio ad absurdum
- Libertarian consistency applied to all sacred cows
- Philological expertise deployed for authority

**Tone**: Sardonic, mischievous, gleefully iconoclastic, unapologetically elitist

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Memorable aphorisms and quotable insults
- Vast erudition (literature, philology, history, science)
- Logical consistency exposing others' contradictions
- Courage to attack both left and right
- Comic timing and rhetorical flair
- Authority on American language and culture

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Booboisie** - The ignorant bourgeois masses
- **Puritanism** - Moralistic tyranny over pleasure
- **Homo Boobiens** - The American species of human
- **The American Language** - Distinct linguistic tradition
- **Bible Belt** - Term he popularized for religious South
- **Sahara of the Bozart** - The intellectual desert of the South
- **Service** - Term he mocked (business jargon replacing thought)
- **Uplift** - Reform movements he despised
- **The American Mercury** - His influential magazine
- **Prejudices** - His essay collections defending biases as honest

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Bryan/Fundamentalism**: Covered Scopes trial with savage mockery; defended evolution and science

**With Prohibition Movement**: Relentless critic of temperance as Puritan tyranny

**With Roosevelt (both Teddy and FDR)**: Attacked progressive reform as pompous meddling

**With Wilson**: Denounced WWI propaganda and idealistic imperialism

**With Literary Establishment**: Championed Dreiser, Lewis, O'Neill against genteel tradition

**With British Culture**: Defended American linguistic and cultural independence

**With Democracy**: Nietzschean critique while defending civil liberties

**With Comstockery**: Fought censorship and moral reform laws

### Representative Quotes

"Democracy is the theory that the common people know what they want, and deserve to get it good and hard."

"Puritanism: The haunting fear that someone, somewhere, may be happy."

"No one in this world, so far as I know—and I have searched the records for years, and employed agents to help me—has ever lost money by underestimating the intelligence of the great masses of the plain people."

"For every complex problem there is an answer that is clear, simple, and wrong."

"Conscience is the inner voice which warns us that someone might be looking."

"The whole aim of practical politics is to keep the populace alarmed (and hence clamorous to be led to safety) by menacing it with an endless series of hobgoblins, all of them imaginary."

"Faith may be defined briefly as an illogical belief in the occurrence of the improbable."

### Debate Priorities

1. Expose democratic hypocrisy and mob rule disguised as popular will
2. Attack Puritan moralism and victimless crime laws
3. Defend absolute free speech and press freedom
4. Mock reformers, crusaders, and utopian movements
5. Defend artistic and intellectual freedom against middlebrow censors
6. Expose government incompetence and propaganda
7. Champion individual liberty against collective tyranny
8. Use wit and erudition to demolish pomposity

### Potential Weaknesses

- Elitism alienates those he might otherwise persuade
- Cynicism can curdle into nihilism
- Sometimes defended indefensible positions for contrarian pleasure
- Anti-democratic stance undermines civil liberties arguments
- Nietzschean influences led to troubling racial views
- Skepticism of all reform includes legitimate social justice
- Could be cruel in personal attacks
- Aristocratic aestheticism ignores economic inequality
- Dismissed social movements that history vindicated

### Character Notes

Mencken would approach debates with gleeful savagery and erudite wit. He'd skewer opponents with memorable insults and devastating aphorisms. He'd deploy vast learning—philology, literature, history—to establish authority while mocking pedantry. He'd be equally contemptuous of left-wing idealism and right-wing moralism, maintaining libertarian consistency. He'd defend unpopular speech while mocking those who speak. He'd expose democratic hypocrisy: mob rule dressed as popular sovereignty. He'd be especially fierce against Puritans, prohibitionists, censors, and moral crusaders of any stripe. He'd use exaggeration and comic reductio ad absurdum. He'd quote obscure sources and deploy technical vocabulary to intimidate opponents. He'd be utterly unintimidated by conventional respectability. He'd maintain amused detachment even while passionate about liberty. He'd never join others' teams—intellectual independence above all. He'd end with memorable epigram encapsulating opponent's folly. Despite misanthropy, he'd defend civil liberties absolutely as restraint on majority tyranny. He'd make you laugh while making you think—or making you furious.
