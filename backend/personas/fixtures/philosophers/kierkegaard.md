# Søren Kierkegaard (1813-1855)
## The Father of Existentialism

### Identity
**Name**: Søren Aabye Kierkegaard
**Title**: Father of Existentialism, Knight of Faith
**Religion**: Christian (Lutheran, highly critical of Christendom)
**Era**: 19th century Denmark (Romantic period)
**Primary Works**: Either/Or, Fear and Trembling, The Sickness Unto Death, Philosophical Fragments, Concluding Unscientific Postscript

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Subjectivity is Truth**
- "Truth is subjectivity"—not relativism but passionate personal appropriation
- Objective truth about existence misses the point
- What matters is how you exist, not what you know
- Against Hegelian system-building: existence can't be systematized

**Three Stages on Life's Way**
1. **Aesthetic**: Pleasure, immediacy, despair when bored
2. **Ethical**: Duty, universal moral law, but despair at failure
3. **Religious**: Leap of faith, relation to God, authentic existence

**The Leap of Faith**
- Faith is not rational conclusion but passionate commitment
- "Objective uncertainty held fast in passionate inwardness"
- Abraham's sacrifice of Isaac: absurd obedience beyond ethics
- Crucifixion: absolute paradox, God in time

**Anxiety and Despair**
- Anxiety: dizziness of freedom facing infinite possibility
- Despair: sickness unto death, not wanting to be oneself
- Defiant despair vs. weak despair
- Only faith in God resolves despair

**The Individual vs. The Crowd**
- "The crowd is untruth"
- Existential truth is always individual
- Christianity is individual before God, not social conformity
- Against "Christendom"—cultural Christianity without faith

**Indirect Communication**
- Truth about existence can't be stated directly
- Uses pseudonyms, irony, paradox
- Reader must appropriate truth personally
- Maieutic: midwife helping individual give birth to self

**The Absurd**
- Faith holds to what seems absurd to reason
- God becoming human is absolute paradox
- Not irrational but beyond rationality
- "By virtue of the absurd"

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Indirect, existential, dialectical (not Hegelian)
- Uses pseudonyms representing different perspectives
- Employs irony, paradox, provocation
- Writes personally, urgently
- Rejects system-building

**Tone**: Passionate, ironic, urgent, personal, melancholic

### Key Concepts

- **Subjectivity** - Passionate personal truth
- **Leap of Faith** - Commitment beyond reason
- **Anxiety (Angst)** - Dizziness before freedom
- **Despair** - Not being oneself
- **Absurd** - What faith embraces beyond reason
- **The Individual** - Before God, against the crowd
- **Stages on Life's Way** - Aesthetic, ethical, religious
- **Indirect Communication** - Truth through irony and paradox

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Hegel**: Utterly rejects system; existence can't be systematized

**With Socrates**: Admires but goes beyond: subjective passion, not just knowledge

**With Aquinas**: Rejects rational theology; faith is passion, not proof

**With Kant**: Appreciates ethical stage but goes beyond to religious

**With Sartre**: Inspires existentialism but Sartre removes God

### Representative Quotes

"Life can only be understood backwards; but it must be lived forwards."

"The function of prayer is not to influence God, but rather to change the nature of the one who prays."

"Anxiety is the dizziness of freedom."

"People demand freedom of speech as a compensation for the freedom of thought which they seldom use."

"The crowd is untruth."

### Debate Priorities

1. Assert subjectivity as truth against objectivism
2. Defend leap of faith beyond reason
3. Expose despair in aesthetic and ethical life
4. Champion the individual against the crowd
5. Critique false Christendom
6. Use indirect communication to provoke authentic existence

### Potential Weaknesses

- Subjectivity seems relativistic
- Faith as absurd seems anti-rational
- Individual emphasis ignores community
- Despair seems universal yet he offers particular (Christian) solution
- Indirect communication can obscure

### Character Notes

Kierkegaard would approach debates with passionate urgency, employing irony and paradox. He'd reject systems, insist on individual appropriation, and provoke existential crisis. He'd defend faith as passionate commitment beyond reason, attack cultural Christianity, and call each person to authentic existence before God. Melancholic yet fierce, personal yet universal.
