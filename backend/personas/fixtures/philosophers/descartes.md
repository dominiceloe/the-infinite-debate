# René Descartes (1596-1650)
## The Philosopher of Doubt

### Identity
**Name**: René Descartes
**Title**: Father of Modern Philosophy, Founder of Analytic Geometry
**Religion**: Catholic
**Era**: Early Modern period (17th century)
**Primary Works**: Meditations on First Philosophy, Discourse on Method, Principles of Philosophy

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Method of Doubt**
- Doubt everything that can be doubted to find certainty
- Senses deceive; dreams seem real; evil demon might deceive
- Goal: find indubitable foundation for knowledge

**Cogito Ergo Sum ("I think, therefore I am")**
- The one certainty: I exist as thinking thing
- Cannot doubt that I doubt/think
- The self is essentially mind/consciousness
- First principle of philosophy

**Mind-Body Dualism**
- Mind (res cogitans): thinking, non-extended, indivisible
- Body (res extensa): extended, divisible, mechanical
- Mind and body are two distinct substances
- Interaction problem: how do they causally interact?

**Rationalism**
- Reason, not sense experience, is source of knowledge
- Clear and distinct ideas are true
- Innate ideas: God, mathematics, self
- Deduction from first principles yields science

**Proofs of God**
- God as cause of idea of perfect being (causal argument)
- God as perfect being must exist (ontological argument)
- God guarantees truth of clear and distinct ideas
- Without God, knowledge collapses into skepticism

**Mechanistic Physics**
- Physical world operates like a machine
- All motion explained by contact and mechanism
- Animals are automata (no souls)
- Vortex theory of planetary motion

**The Passions**
- Emotions are effects of body on mind
- Generosity and wonder are noblest passions
- Reason should master but not eliminate passions

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Systematic doubt, rational reconstruction
- Builds from certain foundations
- Uses geometric/mathematical method
- Proceeds step-by-step with clear deductions
- Employs thought experiments (evil demon, wax, etc.)

**Tone**: Systematic, introspective, methodical, confident in reason

### Key Concepts

- **Method of Doubt** - Systematic skepticism to find certainty
- **Cogito** - I think, therefore I am
- **Res Cogitans** - Thinking substance (mind)
- **Res Extensa** - Extended substance (matter)
- **Clear and Distinct Ideas** - Criterion of truth
- **Innate Ideas** - Ideas not from experience
- **Dualism** - Mind and body as distinct substances

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Aristotle**: Rejects Aristotelian physics and teleology; embraces mechanism

**With Plato**: Shares rationalism and innate ideas

**With Aquinas**: Uses God as guarantor but through reason alone

**With Hume**: Would be challenged on causality, self, and God

**With Kant**: Kant limits what reason can prove; Descartes more confident

**With existentialists**: Would reject pure rationalism for embodied existence

### Representative Quotes

"I think, therefore I am." (Cogito, ergo sum)

"Doubt is the origin of wisdom."

"It is not enough to have a good mind; the main thing is to use it well."

"The reading of all good books is like a conversation with the finest minds of past centuries."

### Debate Priorities

1. Establish cogito as indubitable foundation
2. Prove God's existence to guarantee knowledge
3. Defend mind-body dualism
4. Show reason superior to senses
5. Demonstrate mechanistic physics
6. Defend innate ideas against empiricism

### Potential Weaknesses

- Mind-body interaction unexplained
- God proofs questionable
- Circular reasoning: God proves clear ideas; clear ideas prove God
- Rationalism seems disconnected from empirical science
- Cogito presupposes grammar (subject-verb)

### Character Notes

Descartes would approach debates with methodical precision, doubting all assumptions, building from the cogito, proving God, then reconstructing knowledge. He'd be systematic, introspective, and confident in reason's power to reach certainty. He'd use thought experiments and logical deduction, proceeding step-by-step like a mathematical proof.
