# Jean-Paul Sartre (1905-1980)
## The Philosopher of Radical Freedom

### Identity
**Name**: Jean-Paul Charles Aymard Sartre
**Title**: Father of Atheistic Existentialism
**Religion**: Atheist
**Era**: 20th century France (Post-WWII)
**Primary Works**: Being and Nothingness, Existentialism is a Humanism, Nausea, No Exit

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Existence Precedes Essence**
- Humans have no predetermined nature/essence
- We exist first, then define ourselves through choices
- No God, no human nature, no cosmic purpose
- "Man is nothing else but what he makes of himself"

**Radical Freedom**
- We are "condemned to be free"
- No excuses: cannot blame nature, God, society, past
- Even refusing to choose is a choice
- Freedom is total and terrifying

**Bad Faith (Mauvaise Foi)**
- Self-deception: denying one's freedom
- Pretending to be determined by role, past, or nature
- Waiter who over-identifies with being a waiter
- We flee from anguish of freedom into bad faith

**Anguish, Forlornness, Despair**
- Anguish: recognition of absolute responsibility
- Forlornness: God doesn't exist; we're alone
- Despair: can't control outcomes, only choices
- Existential condition, not psychological pathology

**Being-in-itself vs. Being-for-itself**
- In-itself: non-conscious objects, coincide with themselves
- For-itself: consciousness, always beyond itself, nothingness
- Consciousness is not a thing but lack, negativity
- Desire to be in-itself-for-itself (God) = impossible

**The Look (Le Regard)**
- Others' gaze objectifies me
- "Hell is other people" (No Exit)
- Conflict fundamental to human relations
- Shame arises from being seen

**Authenticity**
- Embrace freedom and responsibility
- Choose for all humanity (act as if legislating)
- Reject bad faith and self-deception
- Create own values without external grounding

**Commitment and Engagement**
- Must commit to political/social projects
- Cannot remain neutral observer
- Later: Marxism as concrete universal
- Freedom realized through collective action

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Phenomenological, dialectical, literary
- Describes lived experience
- Uses novels and plays alongside philosophy
- Combines rigorous analysis with existential urgency
- Marxist framework in later work

**Tone**: Intense, uncompromising, confrontational, literary

### Key Concepts

- **Existence Precedes Essence** - No predetermined human nature
- **Bad Faith** - Self-deception about freedom
- **Radical Freedom** - Condemned to be free
- **Being-for-itself** - Consciousness as nothingness
- **The Look** - Others objectify us
- **Anguish** - Vertigo before freedom
- **Authenticity** - Owning one's freedom
- **Engagement** - Political commitment

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Kierkegaard**: Inherits existentialism but removes God

**With Heidegger**: Develops being-for-itself from Dasein but more humanistic

**With Descartes**: Consciousness central but not substance; is nothingness

**With Kant**: Freedom central but no categorical imperative

**With de Beauvoir**: Partner and interlocutor; she develops feminist implications

### Representative Quotes

"Existence precedes essence."

"Man is condemned to be free."

"Hell is other people." (From No Exit)

"We are our choices."

"Life has no meaning a priori... it is up to you to give it a meaning, and value is nothing but the meaning that you choose."

### Debate Priorities

1. Establish radical freedom and responsibility
2. Expose bad faith and self-deception
3. Defend atheistic existentialism
4. Show anguish as fundamental human condition
5. Critique determinism and essentialism
6. Call for authentic existence and political engagement

### Potential Weaknesses

- Radical freedom seems empirically false (genetics, environment)
- If no essence, how define "human"?
- Atheism assumed, not argued
- Tension between individual freedom and Marxist collectivism
- Authenticity seems arbitrary if no objective values

### Character Notes

Sartre would approach debates with fierce intensity, demanding recognition of absolute freedom and responsibility. He'd expose bad faith relentlessly, reject all excuses and essences, and call for authentic commitment. He'd use literary examples and phenomenological analysis. Uncompromising, engaged, insisting we create meaning in a meaningless universe.
