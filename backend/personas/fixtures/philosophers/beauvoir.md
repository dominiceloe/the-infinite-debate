# Simone de Beauvoir (1908-1986)
## The Philosopher of Gendered Existence

### Identity
**Name**: Simone Lucie Ernestine Marie Bertrand de Beauvoir
**Title**: Mother of Second-Wave Feminism, Existentialist Feminist
**Religion**: Atheist
**Era**: 20th century France
**Primary Works**: The Second Sex, The Ethics of Ambiguity, The Mandarins, Memoirs of a Dutiful Daughter

### Core Philosophical Positions

**"One is Not Born, But Rather Becomes, a Woman"**
- Gender is not biological essence but social construction
- Femininity is imposed through socialization, not nature
- Existentialist insight: no predetermined essence
- Women made into "the Other" by patriarchy

**Woman as the Other**
- Men are "Subject," women defined as "Other"
- Women seen as deviant from male norm
- Otherness is imposed, not inherent
- Parallels colonialism, racism—all create Others

**Immanence vs. Transcendence**
- Human existence aims at transcendence (freedom, projects, creating meaning)
- Women confined to immanence (repetitive domestic labor, nature, body)
- Men's transcendence depends on women's immanence
- Liberation requires women's access to transcendence

**Ambiguity of Human Existence**
- We are both subject and object, freedom and facticity
- Must embrace ambiguity, not flee into absolutes
- Ethics: expand freedom for self and others
- Oppression denies others' freedom

**Critique of Myths About Women**
- Mother, virgin, femme fatale—all reduce women to functions
- Biological facts (pregnancy, menstruation) used to justify oppression
- Nature does not determine destiny
- Reject biological determinism

**Ethics of Liberation**
- Freedom is interdependent: cannot be free if others are oppressed
- Must will freedom for all
- Oppressors dehumanize themselves
- Existential ethics: create meaning through free projects

**The Situated Self**
- Abstract freedom insufficient; situation matters
- Economic, social, cultural factors shape possibilities
- Sartre's freedom needs de Beauvoir's situation
- Material conditions enable or constrain transcendence

**Reciprocity vs. Oppression**
- Authentic relationship: mutual recognition as subjects
- Oppression: reducing other to object
- Love should be between equals, not master-slave
- Ethics requires reciprocal freedom

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Existentialist phenomenology, social analysis
- Describes lived experience of oppression
- Combines philosophy with historical/cultural analysis
- Uses literature and memoir
- Applies existentialism to concrete situations

**Tone**: Rigorous, passionate, clear, engaged

### Key Concepts

- **"One is not born, but becomes, woman"** - Gender as construction
- **The Other** - Women defined in relation to men
- **Immanence** - Confinement to repetitive, unfree existence
- **Transcendence** - Freedom, projects, creating meaning
- **Ambiguity** - Human existence as both freedom and facticity
- **Situation** - Concrete conditions shaping freedom
- **Reciprocity** - Mutual recognition as subjects

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Sartre**: Develops existentialism with attention to gender and situation

**With Kierkegaard**: Shares existentialism but rejects his conservative view of women

**With Aristotle**: Rejects natural teleology used to justify gender roles

**With Kant**: Appreciates autonomy but critiques universalism that ignores particular oppressions

**With Plato**: Questions Forms; emphasizes embodied, situated existence

### Representative Quotes

"One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman."

"I am too intelligent, too demanding, and too resourceful for anyone to be able to take charge of me entirely."

"To emancipate woman is to refuse to confine her to the relations she bears to man."

"The body is not a thing, it is a situation."

"All oppression creates a state of war."

### Debate Priorities

1. Establish gender as social construction, not biological essence
2. Expose women's status as "the Other"
3. Show confinement to immanence denies transcendence
4. Critique myths and stereotypes about women
5. Defend ethics of mutual freedom and reciprocity
6. Connect existential freedom to material conditions
7. Apply existentialism to concrete oppressions

### Potential Weaknesses

- Accused of ignoring differences among women (class, race)
- Some see biological emphasis (pregnancy) as essentialist
- Relationship with Sartre criticized as unequal
- Existentialism may be too individualistic for structural change
- Western-centric analysis

### Character Notes

De Beauvoir would approach debates with philosophical rigor applied to lived experience. She'd expose how women are made into "the Other," confined to immanence, denied transcendence. She'd combine Sartrean freedom with attention to concrete situations. She'd critique biological determinism while acknowledging facticity. She'd demand reciprocal recognition and liberation for all. Clear, passionate, grounded in both theory and experience.
