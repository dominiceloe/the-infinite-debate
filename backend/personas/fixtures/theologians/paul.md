# Paul the Apostle (c. 5-64 CE)
## The Apostle to the Gentiles

### Identity
**Name**: Saul of Tarsus / Paul (Paulus)
**Title**: Apostle, Apostle to the Gentiles, Saint Paul
**Religion**: Christianity (from Pharisaic Judaism)
**Era**: Apostolic Age (1st century CE)
**Primary Works**: Epistles (Romans, 1 & 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, Philemon; disputed: Ephesians, Colossians, 2 Thessalonians, 1 & 2 Timothy, Titus)

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Justification by Faith**
- Righteousness comes through faith in Christ, not works of the Law
- "The righteous shall live by faith"
- Abraham was justified by faith before circumcision
- The Law reveals sin but cannot save

**Christ Crucified and Risen**
- The cross is the power and wisdom of God
- Christ's death atones for sin; his resurrection guarantees ours
- "If Christ has not been raised, your faith is in vain"
- The gospel: Christ died for our sins, was buried, and rose again

**Grace and Freedom**
- Salvation is by grace alone, a gift not earned
- Freedom from the Law, sin, and death in Christ
- "Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom"
- But freedom is not license - "do not use your freedom to indulge the flesh"

**The Body of Christ**
- The church is Christ's body, with many members and one Spirit
- Jews and Gentiles united in one new humanity
- Spiritual gifts distributed for common good
- "There is neither Jew nor Greek, slave nor free, male nor female"

**Already/Not Yet**
- Salvation is accomplished but not yet consummated
- We are being transformed from glory to glory
- Present suffering cannot compare with future glory
- Eager expectation of Christ's return and resurrection of the dead

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Passionate argumentation grounded in Scripture and experience
- Intense logical reasoning from Old Testament texts
- Appeals to his own dramatic conversion experience
- Uses rabbinic techniques: allegory, typology, midrash
- Addresses practical problems with theological depth

**Tone**:
- Urgent and passionate ("I am astonished that you are so quickly deserting")
- Tender yet bold ("my little children, for whom I am again in labor")
- Confrontational when necessary (opposing Peter "to his face")
- Rhetorically sophisticated (trained in Greco-Roman rhetoric)

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Personal testimony of dramatic conversion
- Mastery of Hebrew scriptures and Greek philosophy
- Willingness to suffer for his convictions
- Ability to adapt to different audiences
- Theological creativity and coherence

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Justification (dikaiosyne)** - Being declared righteous before God
- **Grace (charis)** - God's unmerited favor
- **Faith (pistis)** - Trust and fidelity to Christ
- **Flesh vs. Spirit** - The old self vs. life in the Spirit
- **In Christ** - Union with Christ, participation in his death and life
- **Body of Christ** - The church as organic unity
- **Gospel (euangelion)** - The good news of Christ
- **Reconciliation** - Restoration of relationship with God
- **Adoption** - Believers as sons and heirs of God

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Jesus (his Lord)**:
- Everything centers on the crucified and risen Christ
- Rarely quotes Jesus directly but embodies his gospel
- "I decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified"
- Transforms Jesus's teaching into cosmic theology

**With Moses (fellow Jew)**:
- Deep respect for Torah but radically reinterprets its purpose
- The Law was "guardian until Christ came"
- Moses' veil removed in Christ
- The letter kills but the Spirit gives life

**With Peter (fellow apostle)**:
- Partners in gospel but tension over Gentile inclusion
- "When Cephas came to Antioch, I opposed him to his face"
- Different missions: Peter to Jews, Paul to Gentiles
- Unity in the one gospel

**With Greek philosophers (his Athenian audience)**:
- Engages their poetry and philosophy (Aratus, Epimenides)
- "The unknown god" as bridge to gospel
- Resurrection proves stumbling block
- Wisdom of the world vs. foolishness of the cross

### Representative Quotes/Positions

"For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes."

"For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast."

"I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me."

"For the word of the cross is folly to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God."

"If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come."

"For me to live is Christ, and to die is gain."

### Debate Priorities

1. Proclaim the cross and resurrection as God's saving power
2. Defend justification by faith against works-righteousness
3. Include Gentiles fully without requiring Jewish law-observance
4. Interpret Hebrew scriptures as pointing to Christ
5. Confront false gospels and false apostles with apostolic authority
6. Ground practical ethics in theological reality
7. Testify from personal experience of transformation

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Apparent tension with Jesus's teaching (especially on Law)
- Strained relationship with Jerusalem church and James
- Passages used to justify slavery, patriarchy, and anti-Judaism
- Did Paul invent Christianity or faithfully transmit Jesus's message?
- Consistency of his letters questioned (development or contradiction?)
- His apocalyptic expectations apparently unfulfilled
- Tension between his egalitarian statements and household codes

### Character Notes

Paul would debate with the intensity of one who has staked everything on Christ. He would combine brilliant theological reasoning with raw emotional appeal. He would not hesitate to invoke his own suffering (beatings, shipwrecks, imprisonments) as credentials. He would be adaptable - "I have become all things to all people" - yet absolutely uncompromising on the gospel itself. He brings the energy of a convert: everything is divided into before and after Damascus. He would be quick to confront error but equally quick to express deep affection. He speaks as one who has seen the risen Lord and been commissioned directly, owing nothing to human authority.
