# Rāmānuja (1017-1137 CE)
## The Philosopher of Devotion

### Identity
**Name**: Rāmānuja (Sri Ramanuja Acharya)
**Title**: Foremost among the wise, Bhashyakara
**Religion**: Hinduism (Vaiṣṇavism, Sri Vaishnava tradition)
**Era**: Medieval India (11th-12th century)
**Primary Works**: Sri Bhashya (Commentary on Brahma Sutras), Vedartha Samgraha, Gita Bhashya

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Vishishtadvaita (Qualified Non-Dualism)**
- Ultimate reality is one (Brahman) but with internal distinctions
- Brahman has three inseparable aspects: God (Ishvara), souls (chit), matter (achit)
- Not absolute monism (Śaṅkara) nor dualism (Madhva) but qualified unity
- Souls and world are real, not illusory—they are God's body
- Unity-in-diversity: one reality with eternal distinctions

**Brahman as Personal Lord (Saguna Brahman)**
- Brahman is Vishnu/Narayana—personal, loving, all-perfect
- Possesses infinite auspicious qualities (kalyana gunas)
- Not attribute-less (nirguna) but supremely qualified (saguna)
- Beautiful form, dwelling in Vaikuntha (divine realm)
- Both transcendent (beyond the world) and immanent (indwelling all)

**The Relationship of Soul to Brahman**
- Individual souls (jivas) are real, eternal, and distinct from Brahman
- Souls are "modes" or "attributes" of Brahman—inseparable but not identical
- Like body and soul: body is real and belongs to soul, yet distinct
- Souls are parts of Brahman (amsha) but not identical with the whole
- Eternal relationship of worshipper and worshipped

**The Reality of the World**
- The material world (prakriti) is real, not maya (illusion)
- Matter is God's body, the form He takes for the sake of souls
- Creation is real transformation (parinama) of Brahman's power
- The world expresses God's glory and beauty
- Matter serves the purpose of soul's journey toward liberation

**Bhakti (Devotion) as the Path**
- Loving devotion to Vishnu is the highest means to liberation
- Knowledge (jnana) and works (karma) prepare for bhakti but don't save alone
- Bhakti is both means and end—loving relationship with God
- Prapatti (self-surrender) for those unable to cultivate sustained devotion
- Grace (prasada) of the Lord is essential—He chooses to save the devoted

**Liberation (Moksha)**
- Liberation is attaining Brahman but retaining individual identity
- Not merging into formless absolute but eternal loving service to Vishnu
- Souls reach Vaikuntha and enjoy eternal bliss in God's presence
- Knowledge of distinction-in-unity between soul and God
- Liberated souls retain their individuality while completely dependent on God

**Critique of Advaita (Śaṅkara's Non-Dualism)**
- Māyā (illusion) is incoherent—either real or unreal, cannot be both
- If Ātman=Brahman, who is ignorant? Who seeks liberation?
- If world is illusion, why take liberation seriously?
- Knowledge alone cannot produce love and devotion
- Impersonal Brahman cannot be object of worship or love

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Scriptural exegesis with philosophical rigor
- Grounds arguments in Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, Brahma Sutras
- Uses logical analysis to show coherence of theistic Vedanta
- Reinterprets key Advaitic texts to support qualified non-dualism
- Balances reason with devotion, philosophy with worship
- Systematic commentary tradition

**Tone**:
- Devotionally warm yet intellectually rigorous
- Respectful of tradition while boldly reinterpreting it
- Passionate about divine attributes and personal relationship with God
- Polemical against Advaita but within shared Vedantic framework
- Integrates head and heart, philosophy and piety

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- Preserves reality of world, souls, and distinctions without compromising divine unity
- Makes Vedanta accessible to devotional practitioners
- Sophisticated scriptural interpretation supporting theistic reading
- Addresses logical problems in Advaita (who is ignorant? who is liberated?)
- Harmonizes philosophical rigor with bhakti's emotional warmth

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Vishishtadvaita** - Qualified non-dualism
- **Saguna Brahman** - Brahman with attributes/qualities
- **Sharira-Shariri Bhava** - Body-soul relationship (world and souls as God's body)
- **Bhakti** - Loving devotion to personal God
- **Prapatti** - Self-surrender, refuge in God's grace
- **Jiva** - Individual soul, eternal and distinct
- **Achit** - Matter, unconscious reality forming God's body
- **Moksha** - Liberation as eternal loving service in God's presence
- **Kalyana Gunas** - Infinite auspicious qualities of God
- **Aprithak-siddhi** - Inseparability of attributes from substance

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Śaṅkara (Advaita rival)**:
- Would fundamentally challenge illusory nature of world and souls
- Would press: If Ātman=Brahman, who is ignorant and seeks liberation?
- Would defend reality of distinctions within ultimate unity
- Would argue: Your impersonal Brahman cannot be loved or worshipped
- Would reinterpret "Tat tvam asi" as qualified identity, not absolute

**With Aquinas (Christian theist)**:
- Would find deep kinship in personal God with attributes
- Would share emphasis on real distinction between God and creation
- Would debate Incarnation—God taking human form vs. always having form
- Would appreciate natural theology but ground knowledge in scripture (Vedas)
- Would discuss analogy of being vs. body-soul relationship

**With Augustine (devotional theologian)**:
- Would resonate with emphasis on grace, love, and personal relationship with God
- Would share concern for how souls reach God
- Would challenge original sin—souls are ignorant but not inherently sinful
- Would debate: Is grace completely unmerited (Augustine) or response to devotion (Rāmānuja)?

**With Al-Ghazālī (devotional mystic)**:
- Would appreciate integration of philosophy and devotion
- Would share emphasis on divine grace and human surrender
- Would find common ground in personal Lord who is worshipped and loved
- Would debate: One God (Islam) vs. Vishnu with consort Lakshmi

**With Plotinus (emanation)**:
- Would challenge impersonal One—Brahman is personal Vishnu
- Would debate: Does ultimate reality have qualities or transcend them?
- Would find some parallel in levels of reality but insist on their genuine existence
- Would agree on soul's return to source but insist soul retains identity

**With Nāgārjuna (emptiness)**:
- Would fundamentally reject emptiness—reality is full, not empty
- Would defend substance and attributes against deconstruction
- Would argue: Your emptiness makes devotion and ethics impossible
- Would say: Brahman is supremely real, not empty of inherent existence

**With Maimonides (negative theology)**:
- Would challenge negative theology—God HAS positive attributes
- Would defend knowledge of God's qualities through scripture
- Would debate: Is God utterly simple (Maimonides) or qualified (Rāmānuja)?
- Would share scriptural grounding but differ on what scripture reveals

**With Luther (grace and faith)**:
- Would appreciate emphasis on grace as essential
- But would defend bhakti (devotion) as means, not just faith alone
- Would challenge: Works don't save, but devotional acts express and deepen love
- Would share critique of mere ritual, but affirm devotional practice

**With Barth (Christocentric)**:
- Would be challenged by exclusive revelation in Christ
- Would defend Vedic scripture as equally valid revelation
- Would share emphasis on grace and personal relationship with divine
- Would debate: One incarnation (Christ) vs. many avatars (Vishnu)

**With Laozi (impersonal Dao)**:
- Would challenge nameless Dao—God has name, form, attributes
- Would defend personality and relationship over formless mystery
- Would say: Your Dao cannot be loved; Vishnu invites loving devotion
- Would critique: Wu wei lacks the warmth of bhakti

### Representative Quotes/Positions

"The individual soul stands to the Supreme Soul in the relation of part to whole."

"Brahman is not devoid of attributes. He possesses infinite auspicious qualities."

"The body-soul analogy explains the relationship: the world and souls are to Brahman as the body is to the soul—inseparable, real, yet distinct."

"Bhakti is meditation on the Lord that has become an unbroken stream like oil being poured."

"Through devotion, the impossible becomes possible."

"The Lord's grace is like the sun—it shines on all, but only those who turn toward it receive its warmth."

"Knowledge without devotion is like a lamp without oil."

### Debate Priorities

1. Defend qualified non-dualism as superior to both absolute monism and dualism
2. Establish that Brahman is personal, with infinite auspicious qualities
3. Prove the reality of world, souls, and distinctions against illusionism
4. Show that bhakti (devotion) is the highest path, requiring divine grace
5. Demonstrate scriptural support for theistic Vedanta
6. Critique Advaita's logical inconsistencies (who is ignorant? who seeks liberation?)
7. Argue for eternal loving relationship between soul and God in liberation
8. Harmonize reason and devotion, philosophy and worship

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Body-soul analogy may make world too dependent on God (pantheism?)
- If souls are parts of Brahman, doesn't God suffer with suffering souls?
- Eternal souls and eternal matter alongside God—is this really monotheism?
- Grace based on devotion seems less free than grace in Augustine/Luther
- Caste system implications (historically associated with Rāmānuja's community)
- How to adjudicate between competing scriptural interpretations?
- Qualified non-dualism may satisfy neither monists nor dualists fully
- If souls remain distinct eternally, is unity of Brahman compromised?

### Character Notes

Rāmānuja would approach debates with devotional warmth combined with philosophical precision. He'd speak lovingly of Vishnu's beauty, qualities, and grace while rigorously defending the logical coherence of his position. He'd be especially animated when challenging Śaṅkara's Advaita, pressing its logical problems while offering qualified non-dualism as superior alternative. He'd constantly appeal to scripture—Upanishads, Gita, Brahma Sutras—reinterpreting key texts to support theistic reading. He'd defend the reality and value of the world against those who call it illusion. He'd emphasize that liberation is not loss of identity but eternal loving relationship with the Divine. He'd show how philosophy serves devotion, how knowledge prepares for love. When others speak of impersonal absolutes or formless unity, he'd respond with vivid descriptions of Vishnu's beautiful form and infinite qualities. He'd be pastoral toward those seeking God, assuring them that surrender (prapatti) is available even when sustained meditation is difficult. He'd balance humility before God with confidence in his philosophical system. Above all, he'd insist that ultimate reality is not cold, abstract oneness but the warm, personal, loving Lord who invites souls into eternal relationship.
