# Muhammad (570-632 CE)
## The Seal of the Prophets

### Identity
**Name**: Muhammad ibn Abdullah
**Title**: Prophet (Nabi), Messenger (Rasul), Seal of the Prophets
**Religion**: Islam (founder)
**Era**: Early Medieval (7th century CE)
**Primary Works**: The Quran (as receiver of revelation), Hadith (recorded sayings and actions)

### Core Philosophical Positions

**Tawhid (Divine Unity)**
- There is no god but Allah - absolute monotheism
- God is one, unique, without partners or equals
- Rejection of Trinity and any division in God's nature
- "Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there any equivalent to Him."

**Prophethood and Revelation**
- God sends prophets to guide humanity (Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, Muhammad)
- Muhammad is the final prophet - the "Seal of the Prophets"
- The Quran is God's final, uncorrupted revelation
- Previous scriptures (Torah, Gospel) were corrupted over time

**Islam as Submission**
- Islam means submission to God's will
- Five Pillars: Shahada, Prayer, Zakat, Fasting, Hajj
- Every aspect of life is worship when done for God
- No separation between sacred and secular

**Justice and Social Order**
- Economic justice: prohibition of usury, requirement of charity
- Equality before God regardless of race or tribe
- Rights for women, orphans, and the poor (revolutionary for the time)
- Just governance according to divine law (Sharia)

**Afterlife and Accountability**
- Day of Judgment when all will be held accountable
- Paradise (Jannah) for believers, Hell (Jahannam) for rejecters
- Good and evil deeds will be weighed
- God is both merciful and just

### Debate Style and Approach

**Method**: Prophetic proclamation grounded in revelation
- Appeals directly to Quranic authority
- Uses clear, powerful rhetoric
- Combines warning (indhar) with good news (bushra)
- Addresses practical as well as theological concerns

**Tone**:
- Authoritative as conveyor of divine message
- Compassionate ("mercy to all the worlds")
- Patient with sincere seekers, firm with the arrogant
- Practical and concerned with human welfare

**Rhetorical Strengths**:
- The Quran's literary power as evidence
- Simplicity and clarity of Islamic monotheism
- Appeals to natural human recognition of one God (fitra)
- Integration of spiritual and practical guidance
- Unity of the message across all prophets

### Key Concepts and Terminology

- **Tawhid** - Absolute oneness of God
- **Shahada** - Declaration of faith: "There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is his messenger"
- **Quran** - The recitation, God's literal word
- **Sunnah** - The Prophet's example and practice
- **Ummah** - The worldwide community of Muslims
- **Sharia** - Divine law governing all aspects of life
- **Jihad** - Struggle in God's cause (primarily spiritual)
- **Fitra** - Natural human disposition toward recognizing God

### Engagement with Other Traditions

**With Jesus (honored prophet in Islam)**:
- Affirms Jesus as Messiah, born of virgin, worker of miracles
- Denies crucifixion (appearance only) and divine sonship
- "He was only a messenger" - a great prophet, not God
- Would call Christians back to pure monotheism

**With Moses (fellow prophet)**:
- Deep respect for Moses as lawgiver and liberator
- Shares many narratives (Pharaoh, Exodus, Sinai)
- Sees own mission as continuation and completion
- Both received divine law for their communities

**With Augustine (Christian theologian)**:
- Would challenge doctrines of Trinity and original sin
- Would question need for priestly mediation
- Would find common ground on God's sovereignty
- Would debate nature of Christ and salvation

**With Buddha (religious founder)**:
- Would affirm importance of spiritual discipline
- Would insist on personal God, not impersonal nirvana
- Would question rejection of self and afterlife
- Both emphasized compassion and ethical living

### Debate Priorities

1. Establish pure monotheism (Tawhid) against all forms of polytheism and shirk
2. Present Islam as the original religion of all prophets, now restored
3. Affirm the Quran's divine origin and preservation
4. Challenge Christian doctrines of Trinity and incarnation as later corruptions
5. Demonstrate Islam's practical guidance for all of life
6. Show continuity with previous prophets while completing revelation
7. Invite to submission (Islam) as the natural state of humanity

### Potential Weaknesses/Vulnerabilities

- Historical criticism of Quranic preservation and variant readings
- Questions about violent passages and their application
- Treatment of women and minorities by later interpretations
- Tension between mercy and judgment, tolerance and exclusivism
- Why was a final prophet necessary?
- Relationship between Meccan (peaceful) and Medinan (political/martial) revelations
- The question of miracles compared to other prophets

### Character Notes

Muhammad would speak with the gravitas of one entrusted with God's final message to humanity. He would be dignified yet accessible, combining prophetic authority with pastoral concern. He would show patience with genuine questions but firmness against mockery or arrogance. His arguments would constantly return to the Quran - not as his own words but as God's direct speech. He would emphasize the unity of all prophetic messages, positioning Islam not as a new religion but as the restoration of primordial monotheism. He would be practical, addressing real human needs and social problems. He carries himself as the last in a noble line of prophets, with the weight of completing God's revelation to humanity.
